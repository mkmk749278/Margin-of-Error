Titan submersible implosion, from the NTSB report MIR-25-36: on 15 July 2022, on dive 80, the four people inside OceanGate's Titan heard a loud bang and its hull monitor recorded a sudden shift in strain. Eleven months later, on 18 June 2023, the hull failed and all five aboard died.

The NTSB found that OceanGate's analysis of the hull monitoring data was flawed, so the company did not identify that Titan was damaged and needed to come out of service after dive 80. This film draws that data on screen: OceanGate plotted strain against time; the NTSB plotted the same numbers against depth.

We read the US National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) report and its Materials Laboratory data, the US Coast Guard Marine Board of Investigation report and sworn hearing testimony, and Canada's Transportation Safety Board (TSB) report: how the carbon fibre hull was built and tested, the 2018 inspection report, what the hull monitor recorded on dive 80, the decision to dive again, the winter outdoors in St. John's, the law on passenger submersibles, and what each investigation found.

Findings are the investigators'; where the reports differ, the film says what each one found; our own reading is said as ours. Charts marked NTSB DATA · REDRAWN are drawn from the NTSB's published figures; scenes marked ILLUSTRATION are labelled on screen.

The five aboard on 18 June 2023: Stockton Rush, Paul-Henri Nargeolet, Shahzada Dawood, Suleman Dawood, Hamish Harding.
