## Logline
A hull built with almost no margin heard itself crack on its eightieth dive, recorded it on its own sensors, and was
sent back down. The sensors worked. What failed was the way the readings were read, and every decision that
followed.

## The margin, in one line
**Built to a 6,000-metre, 2.25-factor goal; proof-tested to 4,200 metres for twenty minutes; worked at 3,840; failed
at 3,363.** The safety factor ended up below one, and the record shows the date that started.

## Angles (3 considered)
1. **The re-plot (chosen):** the hull's own data, read wrong then read right. Answers the audience's biggest belief
   ("the warning system worked and was ignored", 11.9k likes) with the record's sharper version. Nobody has drawn it.
2. **The shrinking margin (folded in as the spine of Acts II and IV):** 6,000 → 4,200 → 3,840 → 3,363.
3. **The winter (folded into Act III):** a $1,750 cover, a parking lot, 1.4°F to 84.2°F. Strong, but the investigators
   disagree on its weight (USCG "likely accelerated"; NTSB "of unknown origin"), so it can't be the promise.
Rejected: the CEO's character (the 3.2M film's whole angle; we would be the twelfth), the passengers' stories
(respect, and not the record's subject), the game controller (the record shows it wasn't a factor; one line if
asked in comments, not the film).

## Signature moments in full
1. **The margin gauge (5:15, returns 25:00).** One vertical gauge, cyan scale in metres with psi beside it. Each
   number lands on its word: `6,000 M · DESIGN GOAL` (faded, dashed: never built to), `× 2.25` collapsing upward off
   screen, then `4,200 M · TESTED 20 MIN · 01 MAR 2021`, `3,840 M · WORKING DEPTH`. The amber band between 3,840 and 4,200
   labelled `360 M` and, as ours, `RATIO ≈ 1.09 (OUR ARITHMETIC)`. At 25:00 it returns; thirteen ticks at 3,840; a red
   line descends from above the band to `3,363 M · 10:47 · 18 JUN 2023` and the band inverts red.
2. **The re-plot (13:00).** Six dives' strain-gauge-group-4 hoop traces from the NTSB Materials Laboratory report
   (source strip on screen). First against time: six differently shaped humps, no obvious story. The x-axis label
   rotates and morphs from `TIME` to `DEPTH (M)`; each trace re-flows into strain-vs-depth. Dives 75 and 80 settle on
   one straight cyan line. 81, 82, 83 land a beat later and, in the shallowest 500 m, bend away in red. One sentence
   over it, no music: "Against depth, the hull is no longer the same hull after dive eighty." Then the number, said and
   shown as each slope lands: the NTSB's fitted slope for the first 150 metres, `5.00` and `4.74 M/MV` before,
   `8.76 · 8.09 · 8.77 M/MV` after (C41). Then the NTSB's own line. Source: NTSB Materials Laboratory Factual Report
   24-012, figs. 28-31 (docket DCA23FM036).
   *Build depends on the docket's data files or the report figures' digitised values; if only the figures exist,
   values are digitised and the screen says `DIGITISED FROM NTSB FIGURES · RECONSTRUCTION`.*
3. **"Definitely Stockton." (12:30).** The transcript page in desk pose, the question highlighted as it is read, the
   two-word answer alone on a held frame for a full second. If the Coast Guard's hearing video is public and licensed,
   the real moment replaces the page; never re-voiced, never a synthetic voice reading a real person's words as if
   recorded: the narrator reads it as a quotation, introduced as one.

- **Mr. Rush** died in the implosion. Every judgement about him is the board's or the NTSB's, attributed in the same
  sentence; C39 always with its caveat. No adjectives of ours ("reckless", "arrogant").
- **Phil Brooks** and the Director of Marine Operations: public witnesses; their sworn words verbatim, role first;
  nothing about them beyond the record. Brooks's own account is that the shift seemed minor at the time (C34): we say
