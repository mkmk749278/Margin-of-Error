# Viewer panel: *Margin of Error* (titan_review_v2_720p, 23:05)

**How we watched it.** We read contact sheets of all 23:05 at two frames a second next to `titan_narration_timed.txt`, and pulled full-resolution frames at 3:50, 11:56, 14:03, 18:01 and 19:46. We transcribed the whole soundtrack with Whisper (base.en). The narration, and all six REAL HEARING CLIPs, transcribe clean and match the script. A sound bed carries the gaps between lines: about −19 to −23 dB mean, against −16 dB under narration. So nothing below is about audio level. It is about what is on screen and what is said.

**The film's acts, from its own act cards:**
- Cold open (0:00–1:57)
- Act II: The Mechanism (1:57–5:38)
- Act III: The Boardroom (5:38–18:17, which is 12.5 minutes)
- Act IV: The Cascade (18:17–20:16)
- Act V: The Verdict (20:16–23:05)

**Format of the notes:** [time]: what happened → what the viewer felt → the change we would make. "Needs source" marks information we would add but have not checked against the record.

---

## 1. The four viewers

**(1) Dana, US mechanical/materials engineer**
> "The strain-versus-depth plot at 11:10 is the reason to watch this. Nobody else on YouTube redraws the NTSB points and puts the slopes on screen (5.00 / 4.74 → 8.76 / 8.09 / 8.77). But the film never tells me what the change *means* physically. More metres per millivolt is *less* hoop strain per metre near the surface. Is that the gauge's patch carrying less load because a layer has let go? The 13:10 explanation ('a layer can move on its own') doesn't tell me which way the gauge should move. Also, 2:22 lets the CEO's belief that carbon fibre is 'better in compression than in tension' stand with no rebuttal. That's the one physics claim the audience most needs answered."

**(2) Graham, UK commercial pilot, reads AAIB/NTSB reports**
> "This is how I like an accident told: sequence, dated decisions, findings attributed to the right body, and the film's own inference flagged as its own (12:10, 'our own reading … not a finding'). The 19:29 dive-record chart with the decisions alongside is basically a CVR-style timeline, and it's the best summary in the film. What bothered me: at 10:33 Brooks says he looked for 'linearity with depth', and at 10:48 the NTSB's Kramer says OceanGate always plotted against time. Those contradict each other and the film just moves on. I'd also have liked the human-factors finding stated plainly: nobody with structural expertise was in the loop after early 2023. The film implies it at 16:13 but never says it in one line."

**(3) Marcus, US financial analyst, long-form YouTube at a desk**
> "I paused a lot, and the pausing hurt. Rolling counters freeze on numbers that look like data: '+62%' at 11:56.0 before '+75%', '+89%' at 12:58.5 before '+98%', '28.6°F' at 14:40. Two percentages a minute apart, +75% (NTSB) and +98% (TSB), measure different things, and I had to rewind to work that out. On money: CA$1,750 for a cover (14:13) and $1.85 m advanced by the CEO (15:19) are great details. But I never learn the ticket price, how many paying passengers there were, or what a proper hull test or classification would have cost. Without those I can't judge the 'cost over safety' finding at 15:08."

**(4) Sue, UK, watching on the telly across the room**
> "Most of it is small white and orange writing on a dark blue box. From my sofa I could read the big questions ('How would anyone know?', 'Was money behind these decisions?') and the names at the end, not much else. I never saw the submarine itself, or the man who ran it, or the five people. The only face is an old gentleman in a hearing room. The bit with the bang and 'Definitely Stockton' woke me up. The bit about guidance from 1993 lost me."

## 2. Did it give them what they came for, better than the other films?

| Viewer | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Engineer | **Yes, better than any of the listed films on the evidence, short of the physics** | The depth replot (11:10–11:57) and the void evidence (13:14–13:31, NTSB Fig. 17) go past Scott Manley's NTSB summary. But Manley *explains* the mechanism in plain speech, and Two Bit da Vinci animates what an implosion does. This film doesn't explain composite compression behaviour or the direction of the strain change. |
| Pilot | **Yes, the best of the set as an accident report on film** | Careful attribution, three agencies kept apart (18:41 "Here the investigators differ"), its own reading flagged. jeffostroff covers the same USCG/RTM ground in more depth but at far greater length. This film is tighter. |
| Analyst | **Mostly** | It gives the timeline and money details The Fool's character piece doesn't. But on-screen number animations and two unexplained percentages cost it trust at the moment it matters most. |
| TV viewer | **No** | 7NEWS *Fatal Flaws* gives faces, the sub, the families and the search drama. This film is a reading exercise. It would land as radio with captions. |

## 3. Where attention drops, and why

- **[0:41–0:54]** The narration says the NTSB "plotted the same numbers against depth". The caption reads "THE SAME NUMBERS · PLOTTED AGAINST DEPTH", but the chart is still the *time* plot, and the axis label flickers "TIME → DEPTH → TIME" at 0:49. → The engineer and the pilot both noticed the promise wasn't kept. → Either show five seconds of the actual depth plot with the bend circled ("we'll come back to this"), or drop the caption.
- **[2:22–2:41]** Stock robotic pick-and-place footage (Tecnalia watermark visible) runs under the CEO's reasons for carbon fibre. → The engineer: "That's flat-panel layup, nothing to do with a thick cylinder." It reads as filler. → Use the Titan hull build or hull-1 photos from the USCG exhibits (needs source/rights), or the clean hull diagram from 2:06.
- **[2:41–4:37]** One static depth chart for almost two minutes while text piles up beside it. The test table at 3:57–4:25 is unreadable at TV distance (see the 3:50 frame). → The TV viewer gives up. The analyst pauses and is fine. → Split it into three beats: (a) design goal versus 1.25 factor, (b) the models that failed, (c) the pressure-test log. Cut each table down to one big number per beat ("4,200 m: reached once, 20 min").
- **[5:00–5:38]** Near-black frames with small text ("THE SYSTEM HAD LIMITS…"). → Dead air on a TV. → Put the eight sensor dots from 4:42 back on screen and grey out channels 1, 3 and 5 as the narration says it.
- **[9:42–10:30]** Mostly empty dark frames, then an illustrative waveform with a threshold line. → Attention sags right after the film's best human moment. → Shorten by about 15 s. The "under the threshold, or maybe just barely over it" point is strong and needs only the threshold graphic.
- **[14:14–14:35], [15:08–15:35], [16:07–16:38], [17:11–17:20], [18:04–18:17], [20:18–21:14]** Tilted report pages with a highlighted line. This is the film's most repeated device, about 14 times. → By the second half it reads as "more homework". → Keep it for the three findings that matter most (12:00 USCG "all the data it needed", 13:49 NTSB "unable to identify", 20:18 probable cause). Turn the rest into one large pull-quote card.
- **[16:50–18:17]** The regulatory section is all text panels. → The TV viewer and the analyst both drift. The pilot stays. → The 45 m versus 3,840 m bar at 17:57 is the one strong image. Open the section with it, then cover the 1993 guidance in about 20 s.
- **[18:58–19:02]** The final-dive depth counter climbs to 1,280 m and then cuts to a document. → The descent the cold open set up never completes, which felt like an anticlimax and slightly evasive. → Let the counter run to about 3,350 m and stop with the "dropped two wts" line from 1:13, then cut to black. (This is a story beat, not a dramatization.)

## 4. What confused

- **[10:33 vs 10:48]** Brooks: "what I looked for in strain data was the linearity with depth." Kramer: OceanGate's data was "typically and perhaps always plotted as a function of time." → Every viewer asked who is right. → Add one line: Brooks said he looked for nonlinearity with depth, but the plots the company produced were against time (needs source: check CG 112 p. 7xx and NTSB docket for what Brooks actually plotted).
- **[11:56 "+75%"] vs [12:59 "+98%"]** These are two different measures: the NTSB 15–150 m fit versus the TSB near-surface slope against the whole-dive slope. → The analyst thought the agencies disagreed. → Say it: "Canada measured it differently and found nearly double."
- **[Rolling counters]** "+62%" (11:56.0), "+89%" (12:58.5), "28.6°F" (14:40), "1,121 / 1,186 m" (19:01–02). → Pausing desk viewers screenshot the wrong number. → Snap-cut numbers in. Don't count up to them.
- **[7:16]** The narration says "dives fifty to eighty-eight", but the graphic lists only dives 75–80. → Small, but the pilot noticed. → Change the line to "the dives that matter here, seventy-five to eighty-eight", or show 50–88 compressed.
- **[Act III label]** The header "ACT III THE BOARDROOM" sits over the strain-depth reveal (11:10–12:50, see the 11:56 frame) and over the regulator section (17:57). → The engineer: "Why is the data in the boardroom?" → Split into Act III *The Boardroom* (5:38–7:19), a new Act IV *The Data* (7:19–14:03), and Act V *The Winter & the Money* (14:03–18:17). Renumber the rest.
- **[Unnamed people]** Brooks is named. The 2018 director of marine operations and the first director of engineering are not, though both testified publicly. → Viewers who know the case from The Fool or 7NEWS kept expecting names. Those who don't couldn't tell the two "directors" apart (5:46 vs 6:56). → Name them, or explain on screen why they aren't named (needs source: names and roles as given in the USCG MBI report).
- **[13:10–13:31] Physics of the bend** "A layer separates … can move on its own under light pressure." → The engineer couldn't connect this to *less* strain per metre at the gauge. → One sentence from the NTSB materials report on why the gauge saw that change (needs source: NTSB Materials Lab 24-012 / MIR-25-36 discussion).

## 5. What they'll remember a week later

1. "**Definitely Stockton**" (8:50) and "let's dive again and see what happens" (8:54–9:07). Everyone remembered these.
2. **The bending lines** at 11:25–11:57. The engineer, pilot and analyst remembered them. The TV viewer remembered "lines that bent", not what they meant.
3. **The parking lot and the CA$1,750 cover that got no answer** (14:06–14:34). All four remembered it. It's the most quotable fact in the film.
4. "**They had people that paid**" (16:07) and "the safety was just being compromised way too much" (15:58).
5. **The DOJ line** (20:57–21:12). The pilot and the analyst remembered it.
6. The **names in silence** at the open and close. The TV viewer remembered this most.

Nobody remembered the 1993 guidance, the four recommendations (22:00–22:34), or the controller and window myths (19:49–20:16).

## 6. The viewers' real questions the film leaves unanswered

All of these need sources before they go in.

- **What happened at the moment of failure, and how fast?** The film says only "immediate loss of all lives" (21:28). Viewers came from Two Bit da Vinci expecting the physics of an implosion. Source: NTSB/USCG on implosion timescale.
- **The "banging noises" heard during the 2023 search.** This is the most-asked question about the case, and the film skips it entirely. Source: USCG MBI / search-and-rescue findings on the acoustic detections.
- **Did anything on the monitor change during dive 88 itself?** Was dive 88's data recovered? jeffostroff's RTM video covers this ground. Source: NTSB on dive-88 RTM data.
- **Why carbon fibre is a concern for a thick hull under repeated compression.** The CEO's view is given at 2:22 and never answered. Source: NTSB discussion of composite pressure-vessel behaviour and cyclic loading.
- **Ticket price, number of paying guests per season, revenue.** The analyst needs these to weigh the "cost and time-saving" finding. Source: USCG MBI finance section (p. 231ff is on screen at 15:20).
- **Who the five people were.** One line each, at least for the passenger who was 19. Source: public record; handle with care, and families' wishes if known.
- **OceanGate's own position on jurisdiction.** The film gives only the Coast Guard's "reading of the law" (16:53). Did OceanGate argue Titan was outside US inspection rules, e.g. by operating from Canada in international waters? Source: MBI report / hearing testimony.
- **Civil litigation and what happened to OceanGate's people afterwards.** Asked by the analyst; optional for this film. Source needed.

## 7. What the other films do better

- **Two Bit da Vinci:** shows physical intuition for 380 atmospheres and the implosion, and does it quickly. This film's 1:57–2:06 "380×" card is good but brief.
- **The Fool:** gives you Rush as a person, in his own recorded words and on camera. This film has Rush only as text and as a transcript page (6:12–6:20), never as a voice or a face. A desk viewer feels the absence: he's the subject of a DOJ line (20:57) whom they've never seen.
- **7NEWS *Fatal Flaws*:** has faces, the sub, the platform, the families and the search. It is TV grammar, and the TV viewer would pick it.
- **Scott Manley:** a calm explainer talking to camera over the wreck images. He makes engineering legible without on-screen text. This film's text-on-dark approach is clearer for pausing but worse for listening.
- **jeffostroff:** completeness on the USCG report and the RTM. Viewers who want *everything* will still go there. This film wins on clarity and the depth replot.

## 8. The single change that would most improve each act

- **Cold open (0:00–1:57):** keep the promise made at 0:42. Show the real depth plot for five seconds with the dive-81 bend circled, instead of the time plot labelled "depth" (0:41–0:54).
- **Act II, The Mechanism (1:57–5:38):** break the two-minute single chart (2:41–4:37) into three image-led beats. Replace the stock factory footage (2:22–2:41) with Titan's actual hull or a diagram. Add one sourced sentence answering the "better in compression" belief.
- **Act III, The Boardroom (5:38–18:17):** split it. It's 12.5 minutes holding three different stories. Then, in the new data act, reconcile Brooks's "linearity with depth" (10:33) with Kramer's "plotted against time" (10:48) in one line.
- **Act IV, The Cascade (18:17–20:16):** let dive 88 reach about 3,350 m on screen (18:58–19:02). Fix the 19:29–19:49 chart, where decision labels cover dive numbers 75–82 and "≈ 500 M SHORT" is clipped at the right edge (frame at 19:46).
- **Act V, The Verdict (20:16–23:05):** before the closing names, give the five people one human beat each (sourced, restrained), or at least say who they were. Answer the "banging noises" question in 15 s.

## 9. Fairness check

**To OceanGate**
- The film is mostly careful. Findings are attributed (15:08 USCG "troubling prioritization" sits beside 15:16 "The NTSB's findings do not address money"). The film's own reading is flagged (12:10). The spokesperson's statement is included (21:48).
- **Two soft spots:**
  - **[6:12–6:20]** The on-screen transcript highlights crude language from the CEO ("piece of shit", "it's crap"). The narration doesn't need it. The analyst read it as a jab. Consider cropping to the "acoustic monitoring would tell him way before" line, which is the substantive point.
  - **[12:33–12:40]** "Plotted against depth, it would have shown the bend before dive 82." This is flagged as the film's reading, but it's hindsight. Add that the USCG board itself found the data sufficient (12:00), so the claim is backed and not just the filmmakers' own.
- OceanGate's case for why it believed Titan was outside US inspection is never stated (see section 6). Without it, 16:53–17:20 reads one-sided.

**To the regulator**
- Fair, and a strength. 17:21 "The regulator's own guidance gets the same scrutiny" and the 1993 timeline treat the Coast Guard as a party, not only a judge.
- The 45 m versus 3,840 m bar (17:57) is striking. Check that "45 m" is the figure NVIC 05-93 gives as the depth where its rescue is effective, because that's how the card reads (needs source).

**To the people involved**
- **Brooks [7:46–8:02]:** "promoted from software engineer … no background in mechanical engineering or materials" is his own testimony and fair. But the combination with 9:23 ("without being a qualified mechanical engineer…") makes the point twice. It risks looking like the film is blaming the person the company put in the job. Keep one.
- **The contractor (9:29):** she is unnamed and her account is attributed. Fine.
- **The five who died:** they are named respectfully twice, with no images. That's defensible, and probably what families would prefer. But the film never says one of them was a paying passenger's 19-year-old son. Whether to add that is an editorial call. If you add it, source it and keep it to one line.
- **Rush:** the DOJ line (20:57) is quoted accurately and comes with "will not occur, because Mr. Rush died". Fair. Rush never speaks in his own voice in the film, and pairing that silence with the DOJ line is the closest the film gets to judging someone in absentia. If any of the CEO's recorded audio from the 2018 meeting is in the public hearing record, a few seconds of it would balance the film (needs source/rights).
