## 1. Click

| Viewer | Clicks | Scrolls past | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Materials engineer (US) | Main | Challenger 2 | The same data on two axes is what they click for, and "NTSB" signals a primary source. Challenger 2 reads like the fiftieth recap. |
| ROV pilot (UK) | Challenger 1 | Main | Depth bars are a dive log, and "dived it again" is an operations decision. "Re-plotted data" sounds like a lecture. |
| Report analyst (UK) | Main | Challenger 2 | "NTSB" is the authority cue, though "The Damage Was There" sets off the hindsight alarm (see 6). |
| Curious adult (US) | Challenger 2 | Main | A clean revelation with a time span. "Re-plotted" sounds like homework, and "Dive 80" means nothing to them. |

**Thumbnails.** Keep the main image; nobody else has anything like it. At phone size, though, two thin traces are noise. Use one chart: a grey time trace, a fat red depth curve, TIME and DEPTH as the big words, and SAME DATA instead of DIVE 80. In Challenger 1, the shallow dives after 82 read as stubs, and a short red bar for 88 makes five deaths the punchline. Challenger 2's red bracket looks like every other Titan thumbnail. **The best pairing is Challenger 2's framing on the main thumbnail.**

**Titles** (all checkable against the reports):
1. The NTSB Graph That Shows Titan Was Damaged 11 Months Before It Imploded *(lead)*
2. Titan's Own Sensors Recorded the Damage. OceanGate Sent It Back Down. *(A/B test)*
3. Same Data, Different Axis: The Titan Damage OceanGate Missed
4. What Titan's Hull Monitor Recorded on Dive 80, and Why OceanGate Missed It

"Sent it back down" sidesteps "dived" versus "dove", which splits US and UK readers.

## 2. Stay

- **0:45–2:30 (the fatal day):** all four have seen 10:47 and the debris field many times; it's the curious adult's first exit. Cut it to 20 seconds, but keep "first 2023 dive below 33 ft", which is new, damning and currently buried.
- **3:00:** "5,627 psi" is a unit conversion, not a re-hook, and UK viewers don't think in psi. Say "about 380 times surface pressure".
- **5:15–9:00:** about a dozen numbers, then channel counts. This is the curious adult's real exit unless you've teased the depth curve. If 1.09 sits beside 2.25, the engineer reads it as a safety factor, posts a correction and leaves.
- **9:00–11:45 (padding):** the most-covered material in every Titan film. It is about the first hull, and it sits between the setup (the monitor) and the payoff (the re-plot). Give it 45 seconds labelled "Hull 1", and name Lochridge, whom UK viewers know.
- **13:00–20:00:** the payoff promised at 0:30 arrives 12.5 minutes later, too late for two of the four. Then the curves peel, and the explanation waits seven minutes behind the winter and the money. Move 20:00–23:30 straight after the peel.
- **25:00–27:00:** this is a second 10:47 and a second gauge. Merge it into Act V.
- **27:00 onward:** reading the probable cause in full loses all but the analyst. The UK pilot leaves at 30:30, because the status of US Coast Guard rules doesn't affect them. Put the text on screen and voice only the key clause.

About six minutes is padding, so this is a 26–27-minute film.

## 3. Missing questions

1. **Could OceanGate have seen it at the time?** What data did they have between dives 80 and 81, what did they plot, and would their own alert criteria have fired? Without that, "the damage was there" is hindsight.
2. **Why is a shift on 2 of 16 gauges damage, and not a debonded gauge or drift?** What did OceanGate say the bang was? The first engineer comment will ask.
3. **What depth was the dive 80 event at?** The cold open's time trace hides it.
4. **Who was aboard dives 81 and 82?** For the curious adult, paying customers on a damaged hull *is* the story.
5. **How was it legal, and what would a classed operator have done after a bang?** That means "mission specialists", international waters and a Canadian-flagged ship. The comment with 27,256 likes is about regulation, and one line at 0:10 won't answer it.
6. **Did every dive weaken it?** The comment with 4,919 likes expects an answer; the thirteen ticks are the place.
7. **Where did the collapse start, and what do the fragments show?** Show the NTSB's photos, not only animation.
8. **Was "dropping two weights" at 10:47 routine, or an abort?** Did they know? Answer from the record, or say the record can't.
9. **Myths:** a line each on the window, the controller and the search noises.
10. **The UK:** three of the five were British or UK-based. Where do Britain's MAIB and Canada's TSB fit in?

Your 2:30 question, "Where did the margin run out?", gets "neither decided" at 23:30. Ask "When did it start to go?" instead, or warn early that the record can't settle it.

## 4. Versus the existing films

**What they do better:**
- **Two Bit da Vinci:** one clear physical picture for anyone.
- **The Fool:** a protagonist in his own words. With the CEO cut, you have no character unless the dive ledger becomes one.
- **7NEWS and DocuReal:** footage, the search and the human weight.
- **Manley:** a trusted engineer's face. His 2024 video already tells the hull's story, so "every big Titan film tells the CEO's story" is false, and his viewers will say so.
- **jeffostroff:** exhaustive. His viewers may know the monitor's limits, and the NTSB's depth figure has been public since October 2025.

**What is genuinely new:**
- the axis turn as one animated proof;
- the 20-minute proof hold set against 240-minute working holds;
- the two reports side by side where they differ;
- the dated chain after dive 80: the parking lot, the unanswered cover quote, the empty engineering post, the tow, and a fatal dive that was the season's first deep one.

**Your biggest asset is a correction.** The comment with 11,927 likes says the warning system "worked and was just ignored." Your record shows three dead channels, borrowed criteria and misread data, followed by "let's dive again and see what happens." That isn't "ignored". It's worse, and more useful. Say so in the first three minutes.

## 5. Definitive: three changes

1. **Run the hindsight test on screen.** Show what OceanGate had before dive 81, including their explanation of the bang. Draw the depth plot from that week's data, or admit that it couldn't have been drawn. Then show how the NTSB ruled out a sensor fault. That turns assertion into proof, and makes the film fair.
2. **Rebuild around the payoff.** Reach dive 80 by 5:00 and the re-plot by 9:00, with the mechanism straight after. Give the fatal day and 2018–19 under 90 seconds combined. Make the ledger of dives 75–88 the spine, and pin every dated decision to it.
3. **Make it auditable and complete.**
   - Source every exhibit: report, page and figure, or transcript day and page.
   - Show the real NTSB figures and fragment photos beside your animations.
   - Give metric and imperial units throughout (the parking lot ran −17 to 29 °C).
   - Answer questions 4–10 in under three minutes, and name the five.

## 6. Risks

- **Contradictions an engineer will clip:**
  - "heard itself crack" (logline) against "not a through-crack" (22:30);
  - "the sensors worked" against three dead channels and borrowed criteria;
  - "the margin goes negative", when 1.09 is a ratio of test depth to working depth, not a strength margin.

  If the reports give a calculated hull strength, it belongs on the gauge.
- **An unsourced premise:** the whole film rests on "OceanGate read this line against time". If you inferred it from the NTSB's "a more appropriate means", label it as yours, as you did with the 1.09.
- **A sign-off that blames a chart:** "It took a different chart to hear it" sounds like an excuse beside "let's dive again and see what happens", and UK viewers especially will bristle.
- **A single witness:** "Definitely Stockton" is one witness's account of a man who can't answer. Present it as "Brooks testified".
- **Intent:** "to ensure … unhindered" is the board's inference. Quote it with a page number rather than narrating it.
- **The Justice Department line:** "which will not occur" reads as "he escaped justice". Use the board's exact words and let them stand.
- **Respect:** the five are never named. Name them at 0:10 and at the close; Suleman Dawood was 19. Don't animate the collapse with people aboard, and end on their names, not a chart.
- **Speculation:** credit the "layers press together" mechanism to the NTSB, or mark it as yours. Don't let "dropping two weights" imply that the crew knew.
