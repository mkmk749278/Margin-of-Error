## The promise (one sentence; to lock with the treatment)
**Titan's own hull monitor recorded the damage on dive 80, on 15 JUL 2022, eleven months before the implosion; OceanGate
read the data against time, the NTSB re-read it against depth and the change is there; and Titan kept diving.** Case: Titan / OceanGate. Exhibit: the dive 80 acoustic and strain record (OceanGate data, in
the NTSB and USCG reports). Core fact: the NTSB's finding that the hull "likely sustained damage after it surfaced at
the end of dive 80" and that the company's flawed analysis left it "unaware that the Titan was damaged and needed to be
immediately removed from service after dive 80" (C10, C12), with strain read "against time" where "a more appropriate
means ... would have been to evaluate it against depth" (C30), and an acoustic alert tuned on earlier hulls (C31).

## Case sheet (PLAYBOOK §4.3)
| | |
|---|---|
| Corporate decision | **On the record, dated:** 15 JUL 2022, after dive 80 the CEO, "after inspecting the strain and acoustic emission data, told the director of engineering, 'Let's dive again and see what happens'" (NTSB MIR-25-36, PDF p. 36, C13); the hull's insert was never removed to inspect the inner layer (USCG 4.34.8.2, C14); two more dives to the *Titanic*'s depth (C15). 28 JUL 2022, a $1,750 quote for a protective cover went unanswered; the hull stayed uncovered outdoors in St. John's until 06 FEB 2023 (USCG 5.20, C18–C19). The Director of Engineering post left vacant for the 2023 season (USCG 5.19, C21). The USCG board's own words: "financial pressures" and "prioritization of cost-cutting over safety" (C20). **Not on the record:** that storage alone caused the final damage. The NTSB calls the damage after dive 82 "of unknown origin"; the USCG says storage "likely accelerated material degradation". The film says both. |
| Physical failure mode | Carbon-fibre composite cylinder, five co-bonded 1-inch layers; long voids in the interfaces between layers 1/2 and 3/4; delamination at the end of dive 80 (strain response turned non-linear near the surface on dives 81–83); additional damage after dive 82; local buckling at 3,363 m on dive 88 (NTSB, C09–C12). |
| The margin, in numbers | The design goal: 6,000 m with a 2.25 safety factor (C05). The hull that dived: proof-tested to about 4,200 m (6,154 psi) for one 20-minute hold on 01 MAR 2021 (C06), against a working depth of 3,840 m (5,627 psi) (C07). Our arithmetic, said as ours: 360 m of tested headroom, a ratio of about 1.09. |
| Smoking-gun documents | (1) The dive 80 record: AE channel 2 peak 25,687 mV at 3:26:45 p.m., strain group 4 hoop +15.62 mV, longitudinal −30.5 mV (USCG 5.7, C08). (2) The NTSB's before/after strain plots: linear on dives 75 and 80, non-linear near the surface on dives 81–83 (C11). (3) The $1,750 cover quote (C18). |

| Human through-line | OceanGate's **last Director of Engineering**, in role only: told "Let's dive again"; told a contractor the hull had shifted a few "microns"; left in early 2023 because "the safety was being compromised way too much" (USCG 5.19, C22). **Corrections to what everyone says (checked in the record):**
- "OceanGate built Titan from expired carbon fibre bought cheap": **not in either report**; the record has a Toray
  purchase order for 7,700 lbs valued at $332,101 (USCG 4.26.1.2). Dropped.
- "The hull cracked on dive 80": the NTSB says **delamination**, not a through-crack; the deep-depth response stayed
  linear, which is why it survived two more Titanic-depth dives.
- Dive 83's date: Appendix C says 23 JUL 2022, the NTSB's text says 25 JUL 2022. Flag (C16); say neither on screen
  until settled.
- Polar Prince: the USCG findings say *Horizon Arctic* was "unavailable" for 2023 (4.38.1); its analysis calls the
  charter a "decision" (5.20). Both on the record; the film says the first.
