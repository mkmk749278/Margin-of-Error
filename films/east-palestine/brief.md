## Case sheet (PLAYBOOK §4.3)
| | |
|---|---|
| Corporate decision | **On the record:** on 11 FEB 2019 NS announced a plan "targeting an operating ratio of 60 percent by 2021" while it would "implement precision scheduled railroading" and continue "share repurchases using free cash flow and borrowing capacity" (8-K). Average employees fell from 30,456 (2015) to 18,900 (2022); the 2020 executive annual incentive was based on operating ratio and operating income (2021 proxy). The industry's overheated-bearing line (AAR Rule 36, 170°F above ambient) lives in the car repair *billing* rules, set by "balancing between preventing bearing failures and avoiding unnecessary stops" (NTSB p. 106; hearing pp. 215–216). **Not on the record:** that PSR, the headcount or the buybacks caused this derailment. NS did not inspect this car; the NTSB could not determine whether inspection-time goals contributed. The film says so. |
| Physical failure mode | The L1 roller bearing on the 23rd car (hopper GPLX75465) overheated: 38°F above ambient at Sebring (7:37 p.m., no alert), 103°F at Salem with fire on camera (8:13 p.m., below the 170°F crew-alarm line, a quiet alert to a desk), 253°F at East Palestine (8:52 p.m., critical alarm); the axle separated less than a minute later. Below alert to separated in about 30 miles; detectors about 19 miles apart. |
| Smoking-gun document | Two, side by side: **NTSB RIR-24-05 p. 106** ("the AAR definition of an overheated bearing is based on what makes a railcar repair billable to the railcar owner or lessor") and the **hearing transcript, day 2, p. 216**: the NTSB asks whether the thresholds are "a standard at which bearings can be removed from service and billing and the car owners could be billed for it?" — "Correct." |
| Target length | 18–22 min (two stories: the bearing and the vent and burn; the evidence decides). |

**Corrections to the handoff spec found in the record (PLAYBOOK §17.3):** the train was **9,309 feet** (149 cars),
not thirty miles, so the working title goes; the last detector **did** alarm and the crew did respond properly
(finding 1); no source yet for "3 minutes per car to 30 seconds" (the record has an NS email that "appeared to state" a
**90-second** target, and the NTSB could not determine whether it contributed); "PSR demanded by activist hedge funds"
is unsourced (the 2019 plan is NS's own announcement).
