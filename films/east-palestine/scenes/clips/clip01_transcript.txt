Now, going to the issue of how we arrived at those
thresholds. So let’s talk, first of all, what those thresholds
represent. Those are thresholds in AAR’s car repair billing rules
which authorize the ability of a railroad to take a car out of
service, make the repairs that are necessary. Those threshold --
the way in which the thresholds are developed really is a function
of experience, and you’re balancing a couple of considerations.
One of those considerations is, of course, the detection of
issues with the bearings. The other issue, as it was talked about
earlier, is -- you know, if you put the thresholds too low, you’re
going to have a lot of false stops and you’re really going to
bollocks up the railroad system. You’re going to cause issues
with respect to customer service, and you’re eventually going to
lead to shippers choosing other modes of transportation, which
really is not in the interest. It’s really an experience issue in
terms of where to establish those thresholds.
Having said that, as I think some people in the room are
aware, we have been taking a look at those thresholds and will
continue to look at those thresholds. It’s an ongoing exercise in
terms of taking experience into account. But really, that’s what
it comes down to is taking your experience in terms of what
threshold’s appropriate in terms of getting those defective
bearings off the system but at the same time not constantly
stopping trains.
