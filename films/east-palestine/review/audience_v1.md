(2) The film ties this to the decision that a crew only hears about a hot bearing at 170 above ambient, a figure the industry wrote to decide when a repair can be billed to the car owner. **d) Tone.** The hearing clip carries itself, and the narrator mostly stays out of the way. "Hold that thought. The record has an answer, and it is stranger" sounds like a YouTube teaser. "And, among five principles, working safely" has a smirk in it.

**e) Technical statements.**

- **"Below the alert line… in about thirty miles."** Salem read 103, which is above the 90 line, and Salem to East Palestine is nineteen miles. Where did the other eleven miles and the "below the alert line" reading come from? The film never names the detector before Salem.
- **The grease myth.** You say the bearings are "sealed and packed with grease", but you never connect that to the "$7 tube of grease" claim. In my world you don't grease a sealed unit on the line. If that's true here, say it in one sentence. It's the cleanest debunk you have, and you leave it lying there.
- **Missing maintenance history.** When was that bearing last inspected, replaced or reconditioned? A mechanic asks that first. If the record says it, give it. If it doesn't, say it doesn't.
- **"Reportable defects on about a quarter of them, most of them minor."** Compared with what? Without the normal rate for a train, that number floats.

**g) Still open / Act V.** Would a lower crew line have caught this bearing in time? Act V mostly earns its place. The detector-rule ending follows from what came before. The court paragraph doesn't.

## Panel: compared with the existing films

**What the existing films do better:**
- **Human stakes.** All three have residents, sick children, dead fish and town halls. This film has "about two thousand people were told to leave their homes" and nothing more. Viewers who come from the 1.29M-view film will feel the town is missing. One line on scope would help: "This film is about the warning system and the burn decision; health and environmental effects are a separate story."
- **A clear arc and an emotional payoff.** Their villains are simple. This film is more accurate and less satisfying, which is fine, but it needs a sharper central claim to make up for it.
- **Pacing.** The advocacy film moves. This one sags at S15 and S22.

**What this film does that none of them do:**
- It is built on the NTSB final report and the hearing, and uses the industry's own words on camera.
- It explains why the sensor under-read, which is a mechanism none of them touch.
- It traces where the threshold came from (the billing definition), the single most original finding in any film on this case.
- It tests the top viral claims ("cheapest option", "burn destroyed the evidence", "$7 grease", brake rule, crew size) against the record and gives a verdict on each.
- It gives NS's position at length, clears the crew and the help-desk analyst, and notes the car wasn't NS's.

## Top 10 fixes, in order of impact

**1. Close the counterfactual gap, the film's biggest logic hole.**
- Current: "So the line that mattered was one hundred and seventy. Where did it come from?"
- Rewrite: "At Salem the reading, one hundred and three, was over the help-desk line and under the crew line of one hundred and seventy. Had the crew line been lower, the crew would have been told. Whether that would have stopped this train in time, [the NTSB found X / the NTSB did not say]. Either way: where did one hundred and seventy come from?"

**2. Explain the "thirty miles".**
- Current: "This bearing went from below the alert line to a broken axle in about thirty miles."
- Rewrite: "At the detector before Salem, about eleven miles further back, the same bearing read below the ninety-degree alert line. From there to a broken axle: about thirty miles." Confirm the detector's name and reading. Make the same change in the closing paragraph.

**3. Make the title and slogans match what the film proves.**
- Current title: "East Palestine's Alarm Line Came From a Billing Rule". Current line in S21: "One was written for billing."
- Rewrite: Title: "East Palestine's Stop Line Matched a Billing Rule" (the thumbnail can stay). S21: "One matched an industry definition written for billing. The other, investigators could not find in any guidance used at the scene."

**4. Fix the stop-line inconsistency in the cold open.**
- Current: "The line at which a crew is told to stop is one hundred and seventy."
- Rewrite: "The line at which the crew would be told to slow down and stop is one hundred and seventy." Also consider "STOP CHECK AT 170°" on the thumbnail, or keep it and accept the pedantry.

**5. Defuse the S15 juxtaposition.**
- Current: "Norfolk Southern's own plans from those years are in its filings… And, among five principles, working safely."
- Rewrite: "No investigator links what follows to this bearing. But it is how the railroad was being run. In February twenty nineteen, it told investors its plan… Its five principles included working safely." For the bonus line, add the reason for choosing 2020 and whether safety measures appeared in that year or in 2022. For the staffing line, add one context clause about volumes.

**6. Replace the teaser that the film never pays off.**
- Current: "Hold that thought. The record has an answer, and it is stranger."
- Rewrite: "Hold that thought. The record can't confirm it. What it does show is stranger: where the warning line came from." Also, Dana's point: if the record supports it, add "These bearings are sealed at the factory; they are not greased in the yard."

**7. Remove the insinuation about who drew the line.**
- Current: "What the record does show is where the stop line was drawn, and who drew it."
- Rewrite: "What the record does show is where the stop line was drawn: at a number the industry wrote for billing, which Norfolk Southern says it combined with its own experience."

**8. Signal the switch from relative to absolute temperatures, and explain the inversion.**
- Current: "peaked at about one hundred and thirty-eight degrees"
- Rewrite: "peaked at about one hundred and thirty-eight degrees Fahrenheit. This time that's the actual temperature, not a number above the air."
- Current: "to avoid a temperature inversion"
- Rewrite: "to avoid a temperature inversion, a layer of air that can trap smoke near the ground"

**9. Attribute "not invited", and give the email its scope.**
- Current: "Oxy Vinyls was not invited."
- Rewrite: "The shipper, Oxy Vinyls, was not at that meeting. [The NTSB found it was not invited by whom.]"
- Current: "It obtained a Norfolk Southern foreman's email…"
- Rewrite: Add "The email concerned Norfolk Southern's own inspections, not the Illinois yard where car twenty-three was checked." Confirm against the record first.

**10. Untangle the courts paragraph and land the 170 irony.**
- Current: "…the court's order recalled an earlier hearing: this is not a rubber stamp court."
- Rewrite: "…the judge's order repeated words from an earlier hearing: 'this is not a rubber stamp court.'" Use one "as of" date throughout (July or October 2026, not both).
- Current: "the industry's witness said the railroads were now all committed to using one hundred and seventy."
- Add after it: "So after East Palestine, the number written for billing became the line at which this railroad stops a train at once." Check the wording against the testimony.

**Smaller fixes:**
- "We will hear them at about minute seven" → "We will hear them later in this film."
- Cut "No reading. No alert." from S17.
- Cut one of the two hedges in S16.
- Clarify "Thirty-eight seconds" as "thirty-eight seconds after the axle broke."
- "PVC, a hard plastic" → "a common plastic."

## Verdict

The panel's one-sentence verdict: it reads as an investigation, the only one on this case built from the primary record, but the title, the S15 placement and three or four slogan lines ("who drew it", "written for billing", "appeared in none") make it sound like an opinion piece in exactly the places a hostile viewer will quote.
