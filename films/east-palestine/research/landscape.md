## 2. What the record says

**The last 41 minutes** (Salem reading 8:13 p.m. to derailment 8:54 p.m., OUR CALCULATION; RIR-24-05 pp. 5–8, 104–107):
| Time | Where | Reading (above ambient) | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7:37 p.m. | Sebring HBD, MP 79.8 | 38°F | no alert (C03) |
| 8:11–8:14 | Salem cameras | — | fire on wheelset 1 of the hopper car (C04) |
| 8:13 | Salem HBD, MP 69.01 | 103°F | non-critical alert to the Wayside Help Desk (threshold 90°F); below the 170°F crew-alarm line; the crew did not know (C05–C08); the analyst was on higher-priority alerts, and seeing it would not have prevented the derailment (C09) |
| 8:52 | East Palestine HBD, MP 49.81 | 253°F | critical alarm in the cab at about 43 mph (C10–C12) |
| 8:54 | MP 49.5 | — | the bearing separated less than a minute after the alarm; 38 cars derailed (C01, C14) |
The crew handled the train properly (finding 1, C15). The bearing went from below alert to separated in about 30
miles; the detectors were about 19 miles apart (C13, C16). A detector reads the outside of the bearing cup, which lags
the inside (findings 4–5, C17).

**The line itself** (the film's centre): the AAR's overheated bearing (Rule 36: 170°F above ambient, or 95°F above
the opposite bearing) is a car-repair *billing* threshold; railroads choose their own alarm settings "balancing
between preventing bearing failures and avoiding unnecessary stops" (C23–C26). The AAR witness, both sides: "if you
put the thresholds too low, you're going to have a lot of false stops"; "it's in the interest of the operating
railroad not to have derailments attributable to hot bearings" (C27, C29). NTSB: the definition "relates to billing
for repairs, not directly to safety or wayside detection" (p. 107, to add).

**The money** (NS's own filings):
- 11 FEB 2019: the plan targets a 60 percent operating ratio by 2021 (2018: 65.4), "As we implement precision
  scheduled railroading", buybacks continued "using free cash flow and borrowing capacity", "working safely" one of
  five principles (C43–C47).
- Average employees 30,456 (2015) → 24,587 (2019) → 18,900 (2022): −38%, OUR CALCULATION (C48–C49).
- Buybacks $2.78B (2018), $2.10B (2019), $1.44B (2020), $3.39B (2021), $3.11B (2022) per XBRL; about $12.8B in
  five years (2020 and 2022 quoted, C50–C51; 2018, 2019, 2021 to quote from their 10-Ks). Capital spending in the same
  years $1.5–2.0B a year (XBRL).
- The 2020 executive annual incentive: operating ratio and operating income only (C52).

**Where the record stops (say it plainly, as film 1 did):** NS did not inspect this car; it was added at the TRRA
yard (C21), and whether that inspection missed anything cannot be determined (C22). The NTSB found FRA-reportable
defects on 26% of the non-derailed cars (most minor), obtained an NS email that "appeared to state" a 90-second
inspection target, and could not determine whether inspection-time goals contributed (C30–C32); its special
investigation into NS's safety culture (C33) has no published report that we found. **No record links the 2019 plan
to this bearing.** The film shows the documented decisions beside the documented failure and lets the viewer
judge; any reading of ours is labelled as ours (§9.6).

**The second failure: the vent and burn** (pp. x–xi, 15–32): five VCM cars breached at 4:37 p.m. on 06 FEB 2023
(C36); "not necessary" (C37); the shipper's dissent never reached the incident commander (C35). Finding 19: the
DOT-111 cars that started the fire stay legal until 01 MAY 2029, and an early phase-out is "unlikely because of
economic and business disincentives" (C39–C41): a second margin, priced in lease rates.

**Act V threads (status to check at upload):** probable cause (C42); FRA Safety Advisory 2023-01 and supplements
(guidance, not a rule; to quote); no federal detector rule found yet; the $600M class settlement (upheld May 2026, per
press, to confirm on the docket); the federal consent decree (status unclear); the NTSB special investigation; the
pending Union Pacific–Norfolk Southern merger before the STB (application accepted May 2026, per press). After
the accident the Class I questionnaire shows Railroad #2 (13.9-mile spacing, NS's own published average) moving its
critical alarm from 200°F to 170°F above ambient: **our inference that #2 is NS; to confirm** (hearing or NS
submission) before use.

## 3. Our edge, in one sentence
**Every other film fights over the fireball; ours shows the 77 minutes before it (Sebring, 7:37 p.m., to the derailment, 8:54 p.m.): three detector readings, a fire on
camera, and an alarm line that the industry's own witness confirmed comes from a billing rule, set against the
documented decisions of the railroad that ran the train, and exactly where the record stops.**

Signature moments found in the record (for the treatment):
1. **The ladder:** one readout rolling 38° → 103° (amber, the fire on camera beside it, the 170° line above it
   staying dark) → 253° (red); then the axle.
2. **"Correct."** The transcript page on the desk; the NTSB's question highlighted line by line; one word.
3. **The bearing that burns its own evidence:** the root cause "remains unclear", and the NTSB says that is a
   pattern; the honest centre of the film.

## 5. Risks
- **Partisan heat.** Both parties' politicians are in the story; the film names offices only as the record does and
  gives the regulator's choices (no federal detector rule, the 2029 deadline) the same scrutiny as the company's.
- **Pending litigation and the merger:** status and date on screen; no new allegation; settlements are not admissions.
- **Health claims:** out of scope unless from a named, dated study or agency statement; the film is about the margin.
