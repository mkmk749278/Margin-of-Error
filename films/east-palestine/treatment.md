# East Palestine: treatment

Built from the record: NTSB RIR-24-05 (corrected 23 JUL 2024), the NTSB investigative hearing (day 2, 23 JUN 2023),
the docket's Class I wayside questionnaire, Norfolk Southern's 8-K of 11 FEB 2019, its 10-Ks (2014–2025) and its 2021
proxy. Every fact carries its `claims.csv` id (C01–C73; gate passes). Research: `research/landscape.md`.
Working slug `east-palestine`; about 20 minutes (the evidence decides).

## Logline
On 03 FEB 2023 a wheel bearing on the twenty-third car of a 9,309-foot freight train was on fire on a street camera,
and the detector beside the track read it at 103°F above ambient: below the line that tells a crew to stop. The line
came from a rule written for billing repairs. Thirty-nine minutes later the bearing read 253°F, the highest number that
detector could show, and the axle broke away. Three days later a second number, found in no written guidance, set a
13-minute clock on the decision to blow open five tank cars.

## The margin, in one line
The physical margin was the distance between a bearing starting to fail and the train stopping. It was spent by an
alarm line set by "balancing between preventing bearing failures and avoiding unnecessary stops" (C24), from a
definition that "relates to billing for repairs, not directly to safety" (C72), with detectors about 19 miles apart on a
failure that took about 30 (C13, C16). The film is about who sets that line, and what it costs to move it.

## Angles considered
1. **Two numbers** (chosen spine): the film is told through two numbers that decided a town's week. Night one: 170°F,
   the crew-alarm line, from a billing definition (C07, C25–C28). Day four: 153–158°F, the runaway range the
   incident commander was given, which the NTSB found in no written guidance (C63–C64). Both are thresholds; both
   were followed; neither came from the physics of that moment. It is our channel's thesis in one case: accounting
   meets the laws of physics, at a line someone chose.
2. **The 77 minutes** (chosen as the structure device): Sebring 7:37 → Salem 8:13 → East Palestine 8:52 → 8:54. A route
   line with the three detectors and their mile posts runs through Acts I, II and IV and returns in the sign-off.
3. **The railroad that ran lean** (folded into Act III, never the spine): the 2019 plan, the 60 percent operating
   ratio, −38% employees, $12.8B of buybacks, a bonus based on the ratio. True and documented, but **no record links it
   to this bearing**, and this car was never inspected by NS (C21). Alone it becomes the advocacy film that already
   exists; inside angle 1 it is the context the viewer needs to judge "avoiding unnecessary stops" themselves.

## What we can and cannot say (fairness, PLAYBOOK §9.6; full pass at narration)
- **Can:** every NTSB finding as the NTSB's; the AAR witness's words and NS's testimony, attributed, both sides
  together (C26–C29, C73); NS's own filed numbers and words, with "working safely" beside the cost targets (C43–C47);
  the incident commander's own account and his own words that he bore the responsibility (C62, C68).
- **Cannot:** that PSR, headcount or buybacks caused the derailment; that the vent and burn was done "to save money",
  "to reopen the line" or "to destroy evidence" (none of it is in the record); any motive. Act III says, in our own
  voice, labelled as ours: "No investigator has linked this plan to this bearing. What the record shows is where the
  line was drawn, and who drew it."
- **Individuals:** none named except in public role where the record does (the NS chief executive's presence in the
  room, C61, as a fact, not a charge). Witnesses at the hearing by role ("the A - A - R's vice president of safety").
- **Pending:** the class settlement, the federal consent decree, the NTSB special investigation (C33) and the
  merger: status and date on screen, checked on upload day.
- **Partisan heat:** governors appear only as the record places them in the room; no party, no politician's quote.

## Feelings
Start: cold precision, a readout and a burning wheel that nobody in the cab can see. Darkest: "Correct." End: the
unease that both numbers were followed exactly, and that the bearing destroyed the evidence of its own failure.

## Cold opens (3)
1. **The quiet alert** (chosen): no music; a readout on the desk, `20:13 EST · SALEM, OHIO · MP 69.01`; beside it a
   RECONSTRUCTION of the camera's view, the wheelset burning (after NTSB fig. 5; the real frame only if its owner
   clears it). The readout rolls `38 → 103°F` and stops; above it a dark line labelled `CREW ALARM 170°F` that never
   lights. One sentence: "At thirteen minutes past eight, a wheel bearing on the twenty-third car was on fire. The
   detector read one hundred and three degrees. The alarm that stops a train was set at one hundred and seventy."
   *Why chosen:* it is the paradox in five seconds: you see the fire, the machine says "fine".
2. **The cab alarm as text** (C53): `CRITICAL ALARM, CRITICAL ALARM, CRITICAL ALARM.` typing out against
   `20:52 EST`, the readout at its ceiling `253°F`, then the clock runs back 39 minutes to Salem. Strong, but it opens
   on the answer, not the question.
3. **The dual split** (PLAYBOOK §5.1): left, the hearing transcript's one word `Correct.`; right, the 103 readout under
   the 170 line. A low hum, one click. Kept: its two halves become Signature 2 and the cold open.

## Signature moments
1. **The ladder** (12:30, planted at 0:00, 1:10, 5:10): one readout and the route line. Sebring `38°F` cyan (no
   alert); Salem `103°F` amber, the fire beside it, the 170 line above staying dark, an arrow to a desk, not the cab;
   East Palestine `253°F` red, the number hitting its bracket `MAX` (C55); then "less than a minute" counted in real
   seconds. The viewer has seen every number before; now they see them in one move.
2. **"Correct."** (6:45): the NTSB's question highlighted on the real transcript page, phrase by phrase on the
   narration's words ("a standard … at which bearings can be removed from service … and the car owners could be
   billed"), then the answer alone on the page. Room tone only. No comment from us. Then, immediately, the AAR's
   other sentence: "it's in the interest of the operating railroad not to have derailments" (C29). The fairness is
   part of the force.
3. **The second number** (14:50): the hottest VCM car's temperature on a readout, falling `138 → 126°F` (C65); a band
   drawn above it, `153–158°F`, amber, dashed, with its source strip: `NOT FOUND IN WRITTEN GUIDANCE · NTSB RIR-24-05
   p. 28 n. 55`; a `13:00` clock counting down for the decision (C62); the `3:00 P.M. DEADLINE` card, then the real
   time of the charges, `16:37` (C66). Then the rhyme, said once: "Two numbers decided this town's week. One was written
   for billing. The other was found in no guidance."
