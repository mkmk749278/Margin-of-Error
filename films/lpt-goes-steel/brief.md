## Case sheet (PLAYBOOK §4.3)
| | |
|---|---|
| The decision | **On the record:** on 15 OCT 2020 Commerce found that imported transformer laminations and cores "threaten to impair the national security", and recorded transformer makers' concern that the only US maker's steel is not wide enough for the cores of larger transformers (C13, C16). On 02 NOV 2020 Cleveland-Cliffs announced a Section 232 remedy on cores was coming (C28). On 05 NOV 2020 USTR announced that imports from Mexico would not be subject to any such action, in light of a Mexican monitoring regime (C17). Until 18 AUG 2025 no Section 232 tariff covered imported cores (C52, C51). **Not on the record:** why no tariff on cores followed between 2020 and 2025; who lobbied; whether a tariff would have helped. The film says so. |
| The physical limit | Cleveland-Cliffs' domain-refined steel: 920 mm maximum master coil (C07), where the 232 report records transformer makers' concern that larger cores need wider than 932 mm (C16); its laser domain refinement "is eradicated upon annealing at temperatures greater than 600 °C" (C08), and Commerce found no known US production of the heat-proof kind wound cores need; about 75% of wound-core purchases were imported in 2019 (C66, C72). |
| Smoking-gun documents | Two, side by side: **the Section 232 report, the width sentence** ("does not produce steel wide enough (>932mm)", C16) and **the USTR statement of 05 NOV 2020** ("will not be subject to any action", C17). |
| Target length | About 17 minutes (argued beat by beat in the treatment). |

## Package (LOCKED: owner approved with the treatment, 27 NOV 2026; three pairs, one promise)
**The promise:** America's only transformer-steel mill makes coils no wider than 920 mm; in 2020 the Commerce
Department's national-security report recorded transformer makers' concern that larger cores need steel wider than
932 mm, and found that the import dependence threatened national security. The film shows the steel, the finding and the five years that followed.
