## Case sheet (PLAYBOOK §4.3)
| | |
|---|---|
| The decision | **On the record:** on 15 OCT 2020 Commerce found that imported transformer laminations and cores "threaten to impair the national security" and that the only US maker's steel is not wide enough for the cores of larger transformers (C13, C16). On 02 NOV 2020 Cleveland-Cliffs announced a Section 232 remedy on cores was coming (C28). On 05 NOV 2020 USTR excluded Mexico from any such action in exchange for a monitoring regime (C17). Laminations and cores came under the steel tariff only on 18 AUG 2025 (C18). **Not on the record:** why no tariff on cores followed between 2020 and 2025; who lobbied; whether a tariff would have helped. The film says so. |
| The physical limit | Cleveland-Cliffs' domain-refined steel: 920 mm maximum master coil (C07), where Commerce says larger cores need wider than 932 mm (C16); its laser domain refinement "is eradicated upon annealing at temperatures greater than 600 °C" (C08), and wound cores, which are annealed, were 100% imported in 2019 (C26). |
| Smoking-gun documents | Two, side by side: **the Section 232 report, the width sentence** ("does not produce steel wide enough (>932mm)", C16) and **the USTR statement of 05 NOV 2020** ("will not be subject to any action", C17). |
| Target length | About 17 minutes (argued beat by beat in the treatment). |

## Package (LOCKED: owner approved with the treatment, 27 NOV 2026; three pairs, one promise)
**The promise:** America's only transformer-steel mill makes coils no wider than 920 mm, and in 2020 the government's
own investigators found that larger transformer cores need steel wider than 932 mm and that the import dependence
threatened national security. The film shows the steel, the finding and the five years that followed.
