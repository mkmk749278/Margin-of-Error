## 2. What the record says (the spine)
1. **The load:** 90% of US electricity passes through an LPT (C01). 4,900–6,799 in service (C03). In 2019, 137 built
   here and 617 imported (C02). Lead times up to 60 months (C04). About three heavy cars on the continent and nine
   months of route clearance to move one (C05).
2. **The myth audit:** the "38 to 40 years old" fleet figure in DOE 2014 rests on footnote 95: testimony at a 2011
   USITC antidumping hearing, not a federal census (C06). It is repeated to this day.
3. **The steel, in the company's own words:** 920 mm maximum master coil (C07). Laser domain refinement "eradicated"
   by annealing above 600 °C (C08). That matters for wound distribution cores, which are annealed after winding.
   1.01 W/kg for the 0.23 mm DR grade before annealing (C09): good steel for stacked cores.
4. **The national-security finding:** Commerce's Section 232 report (dated 15 OCT 2020) found imports of laminations
   and cores threatened to impair national security. Canada and Mexico supplied over 95% of them, and neither makes
   GOES (C13, C14). Domestic LPT makers need steel wider than 932 mm (C16). Option 1 was negotiation, option 2 tariffs
   (C15).
5. **The dated decision (now on record from both sides):**
   - 15 OCT 2020: Commerce finds cores "threaten to impair the national security" (C13); Cleveland-Cliffs' domain-refined
     steel tops out at 920 mm, "not wide enough (>932mm)" for the cores of larger transformers (C16); core imports up
     650% from 2015 to 2019, 97% from Canada and Mexico (C23).
   - 02 NOV 2020: Cleveland-Cliffs announces the administration "will be moving forward with a Section 232 action"
     covering laminations and cores (C28). The same release argues that the US needs only 1,400 tons of GOES wider than
     920 mm a year (C29): the company's side of the width question.
   - 05 NOV 2020: USTR excludes Mexico from any such action, in light of a monitoring regime (C17).
   - 18 AUG 2025: laminations and cores become subject to the 50% steel tariff, "effective today" (C18, company release
     and Federal Register). Until then no tariff covered them; the think tank counts core imports at $126M in 2018 and
     $524M in 2025 (C21).
   - Meanwhile, DOE (2024): the highest-grade domain-refined steel "is not manufactured in the U.S."; wound cores were
     100% imported in 2019 (C25, C26). A competitor, Howard Industries, says Cleveland-Cliffs "does not produce enough"
     (C30, an allegation, with the company's answer).
   Three administrations held the file. Each gets the same scrutiny. We say what each record shows and draw no motive
   (Gemini's lobbying explanation is rejected: no record).
6. **The collision:** JHU (Yao and Dvorkin, NOV 2026) projects an almost 15% transformer shortfall by 2030 under high
   demand, about 25% with a trade shock (C22, to verify from the paper).
7. **The other side, in full:** Cleveland-Cliffs' $200M Butler Works investment with a $75M DOE award, up to +25% GOES
   volume (C11), about 1,200 UAW jobs (C12). The completion date conflicts between sources (early 2028 vs DOE 2029).
   A modern wide mill is put at $1–2B (think tank; to verify).

## 3. Our edge, in one sentence
**In October 2020 the government's own investigators found that America's transformer cores depended on imports that
threatened national security, and on a mill whose steel is too narrow for the biggest cores; three weeks later the
choice was a monitoring regime instead of a tariff, and the cores kept crossing the border duty-free for almost five
years, while the grid's demand doubled.** Nobody in the scan opens the 232 report, dates that decision, or traces the
"40-year-old fleet" to its footnote.

Caveat, said plainly: "the margin" here is supply, not a single failure. The case still needs an **Act I incident on
the record**: a real transformer loss whose replacement wait is documented (utility filing, PUC record, NESO or FERC
report). Candidates to research next: a US utility's documented LPT failure and wait (PUC filings); the North Hyde
substation fire that closed Heathrow on 21 MAR 2025 (NESO and Ofgem reports, which would give UK viewers their way in).
Without one, the cold open is the demand collision (JHU) and the 232 page, which is weaker.

## 6. Act I and human through-line search (27 NOV 2026)
- **US documented LPT failure with a long wait:** none found on the public record yet. Met-Ed Lebanon PA (JUN 2024):
  failed and could not be repaired; a spare was swapped in by SEP 2024 (utility release). Shows the spare system
  working, not the wait.
- **North Hyde, 20 MAR 2025 (UK):** NESO final review (77 pp, opened): SGT3's bushing gave a category 1 moisture reading
  in JUL 2018, which NGET guidance says indicates "an imminent fault and that the bushing should be replaced"; the
  mitigations "were not actioned"; in 2022 "a decision was taken to defer basic maintenance on SGT3"; 71,655 customers
  lost power and Heathrow closed. This is a complete Track A case in its own right (dated warning, dated decision,
  consequence, official finding), and YouTube coverage is thin (news clips; one explainer at 11.8K). **Recommended as
  its own film, not spent as this film's cold open.**
