## 4. US & UK Legal / Fairness Audit (Defamation, Attribution, and Political Neutrality)

The fairness architecture in `treatment.md` is remarkably disciplined—especially cutting the unverified lobbying causation, dropping the 90-day expiry speculation, banning the think tank's `"wrong steel"` adjective from the studio's voice, and quoting Cleveland-Cliffs' `1,400 tons` rebuttal (`C29`) alongside Commerce's `>932 mm` finding (`C16`).

Here are the **4 specific beats** where a US or UK media lawyer would ask for a surgical wording guardrail during narration writing:

1. **Beat `5:40` (Act II — `"the wound core, annealed after winding, is where it fails"`):**
   - **Legal Risk:** In UK/US product-disparagement law, saying a manufacturer's product `"fails"` can be misread as saying the steel itself is defective or suffers a mechanical failure in service.
   - **Exact Fix:** Replace `"where it fails"` with the datasheet's exact physical verb: *"where its laser treatment erases above six hundred degrees"* (or *"where the laser domain refinement does not survive the anneal,"* per `C08`: `"is eradicated upon annealing at temperatures greater than 600 °C"`). Furthermore, note cleanly that Cleveland-Cliffs' non-laser-scribed conventional grades (`M-2/M-3/H-0`) *can* be annealed for wound cores, just at higher core loss (`C20`).
2. **Beat `7:00` (Act III — `"97% Canada and Mexico, neither makes this steel"`):**
   - **Legal / Factual Risk:** In `86 FR 64608` (`C23`), Commerce confirms `"neither Mexico nor Canada has indigenous production capability for GOES"`. However, as Howard Industries noted in `BIS-2025-0023-0784` (quoting ERMCO's filing), some U.S. transformer makers also exported *Cleveland-Cliffs' own U.S.-melted GOES* to Canada to be slit/wound into cores and re-imported. If `7:00` implies 100% of cores coming from Canada and Mexico contained Asian/Russian steel, Cleveland-Cliffs or Canadian core fabricators could object.
   - **Exact Fix:** Use Commerce's exact attribution at `7:00`: *"According to the Commerce report, Canada and Mexico accounted for ninety-seven percent of imported cores, and neither country produces its own transformer steel—importing their steel primarily from Japan, as well as China, Russia, and South Korea, along with U.S. steel sent across the border for slitting."* (And note on August 18, 2025 [`90 FR 40326` / `Proclamation 10947`], the 50% derivative tariff applies to the non-U.S.-melted steel content).
3. **Beat `8:20` (Act III, Signature 2 — `02 NOV 2020` Cliffs Release vs. `05 NOV 2020` USTR Statement):**
   - **Legal / Fairness Risk:** Placing Cleveland-Cliffs' `02 NOV 2020` release (*"President Trump's Administration will be moving forward with a Section 232 action implementing a remedy covering imported laminations and cores"*, `C28`) three days before USTR's `05 NOV 2020` Mexico exclusion (`C17`) is a devastating documentary juxtaposition. To ensure it cannot be read as accusing Cleveland-Cliffs of misleading investors on November 2, 2020, keep the narration strictly documentary.
   - **Exact Fix:** State only what each document says on its face: on November 2, 2020, Cleveland-Cliffs published a release thanking the administration for moving forward on cores and laminations (`C28`); three days later, on November 5, 2020, USTR published its agreement with Mexico establishing a monitoring regime and excluding Mexican transformers and parts from Section 232 action (`C17`). Zero commentary on why the two announcements diverged.
4. **Beat `14:00` (Act V — Cleveland-Cliffs' `\$200M` Butler Works Expansion: `"what the record does not say: that it widens the coil or adds heat-proof refinement"`):**
   - **Legal / Fairness Risk:** Totally factual and fair to note what the October 5, 2026 release (`C11`) does and does not state—just phrase it strictly as what the public release specifies (*"The October twenty twenty-six announcement specifies new induction reheat furnaces and up to twenty-five percent more volume by early twenty twenty-eight; it does not announce a change to the mill's nine-hundred-and-twenty-millimetre maximum coil width or permanent groove-etched domain refinement"*), so it never implies secret knowledge of internal plant blueprints.

## 5. Missing Details & Unanswered Viewer Questions to Close Before Narration

1. **Why Can't Butler Works Just Buy Wider Rollers? (Beat `3:40` or `15:50` — 1 Sentence Needed):**
   - Every engineer and curious viewer watching `3:40` (`920 mm` vs `>932 mm`) will ask: *"It's only 12 millimetres—half an inch! Why can't a steel mill just roll the sheet 12 millimetres wider?"*
   - **The Fix (from the record):** Add **one sentence** at `3:40` or `15:50` explaining that a rolling mill's maximum strip width is physically fixed by the barrel width of every stand in its hot-strip mill, cold-rolling tandem mill, decarburizing line, and high-temperature box-annealing bell furnaces—widening a coil by even 50–100 mm requires rebuilding the entire continuous line (`\$1–2B`, `C33`).
2. **What Does `"1,400 tons"` (`C29`) Mean in Actual Transformers? (Beat `9:30` — Unit Translation Needed):**
   - At `9:30`, Cleveland-Cliffs argues `"The U.S. market only requires 1,400 tons of GOES in that width or greater"` (`C29`), while Commerce says larger cores need `>932 mm` (`C16`). Viewers have no intuitive sense of whether `1,400 tons` of steel is 5 transformers or 5,000.
   - **The Fix (from the record):** Since a single 100–400 ton LPT contains roughly 30 to 100 tons of core steel (`C22`: `1,030 tons of steel per GVA`, meaning ~100 tons per 100 MVA unit, and `C02`: only `137` LPTs were built domestically in 2019), `1,400 tons` of ultra-wide steel represents only a few dozen of the largest domestically built LPT legs per year—which makes **both** statements intelligible at once: for a steel mill running 250,000 tons/year, 1,400 tons is a tiny commercial niche (<1% of volume), yet for the grid's largest flagship transformers, those few dozen units are the backbone of the bulk power system. Explaining that scale mismatch makes Beat `9:30` an unforgettable "Where accounting meets the laws of physics" moment!
3. **Clarify the August 18, 2025 Tariff Mechanism at Beat `10:00` (`C18`):**
   - At `10:00`, when cores and laminations come under the 50% Section 232 steel tariff on August 18, 2025 (`90 FR 40326`), note in one clause that under the Section 232 derivative rules, the 50% tariff applies to the **value of the non-U.S.-melted steel content** inside the imported core or lamination.
4. **Verify the Sign-Off 1 `"Decades"` Source (`16:20`):**
   - In `treatment.md`, Sign-Off 1 notes: *"(the decades line needs a source or goes)"*. You already have a rock-solid primary federal source in `claims.csv`: the **U.S. DOE July 2024 Report (`EXEC-2022-001242`, p. 5)** states verbatim: *"LPTs are typically considered to have a design lifetime on the order of 40 years"*, and the **U.S. DOE April 4, 2024 release** states distribution transformers remain *"in use for many decades"*. Citing DOE July 2024 p. 5 locks Sign-Off 1 (`"Transformers ordered today are built to run for four decades..."`) 100% to the federal record.
