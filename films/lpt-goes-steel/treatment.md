## Logline
Ninety percent of America's electricity passes through a large power transformer (C01). Only one mill in America makes
the steel for transformer cores, and its widest coil is 920 millimetres (C10, C07); the makers of large power
transformers mostly import theirs from Japan and South Korea (C93). In October 2020 the Commerce Department's
national-security report found the country's dependence on imported cores threatened national security, and recorded
transformer makers' concern that the domestic steel is not wide enough, below 932 mm, for larger cores (C13, C16).
Three weeks later the US Trade Representative announced that cores from Mexico would not be subject to any Section 232
action, in light of a Mexican monitoring regime (C17). Until August 2025 no Section 232 tariff covered imported cores
(C52). Meanwhile the wait for a distribution transformer went from months to up to two years, for three reasons DOE
named: labor, demand outrunning supply, and materials (C34, C59).

## The margin, in one line
The margin was the ability to build the grid's transformers from steel made at home. It rested on one mill whose widest
coil is 920 mm and whose laser treatment does not survive the annealing that wound cores need (C07, C08, C66, C67), so
the cores, and most of the steel for large ones, came from abroad (C72, C93).

## Angles (3 considered; 1 chosen, 2 kept as devices)
1. **The 12 millimetres** (spine, chosen): one width, one temperature, one report. The physics explains why more of the
   same steel does not close the gap; the record dates when the government was told.
2. **The five years** (Act III device): a calendar from 15 OCT 2020 to 18 AUG 2025, walked page by page, with every
   federal action in it, tariff or not (C50, C57, C58, C60). Three administrations held the file (C89): told as dates
   and documents, never as blame.
3. **The waiting list** (Act IV device): distribution transformers from 2–4 months to up to two years (C34), large ones
   up to 60 months (C04), the 2030 model (C22). The search angle; not the spine.

## Fairness (full pass at narration, PLAYBOOK §9.6)
- **Cleveland-Cliffs is a company with a documented position, not a villain.** Its steel in its own framing: the
  domain-refined grades suit transformers whose cores are not stress-relief annealed (C67), typical loss 1.01 W/kg
  (C09). The $200M project, with DOE support of up to $75M paid in phases (C11, C60); about 1,200 jobs at the facility
  (C12). Its own case on width: "The U.S. market only requires 1,400 tons of GOES in that width or greater" (C29, no
  period given), set beside the 232 report (C16). Never "good", "bad", "wrong", "inferior", "defective" in our voice;
  DOE's "no efficiency benefit" and "outdated", and the think tank's "inferior", only as theirs, with context (C25, C87).
- **Howard Industries** is a transformer maker that buys this steel, opposing a tariff request in the BIS docket (C75):
  its "does not produce enough" (C30) is said as its filing, with Cleveland-Cliffs' answer from its own filing (C74).
  Never Howard's stronger words.
- **"Loophole", "circumvention", "transshipment"** are other people's words: Cleveland-Cliffs', the think tank's, USTR's
  ("transshipment", C17). Canada's answer is said (C48).
- **The 2020–2025 gap:** "Until August 2025, no Section 232 tariff covered imported cores" (C52), and the other federal
  actions in the window on the same calendar (C50, C57, C58, C60). No motive, no lobbying claim, no "nobody acted".
  Officials by office; no party names.
- **Both policy views:** Cleveland-Cliffs applauded the 2025 tariff (C18); the Breakthrough Institute argues for
  "immediate tariff relaxation" and investment (C33). Neither is ours.
- **No China-vs-West framing**; countries only as the record names them.
- Terms used exactly: grain-oriented electrical steel (once, then "transformer steel"), domain refinement, stress relief
  annealing, stacked and wound cores, Section 232 (glossed once: "the law that lets the President restrict imports after
  the Commerce Department finds they threaten national security", C83).
