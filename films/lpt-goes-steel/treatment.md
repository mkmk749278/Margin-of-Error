## Logline
Ninety percent of America's electricity passes through a large power transformer (C01), and the steel at its core is
made in the US by one mill, whose coils stop at 920 millimetres (C07, C10). In October 2020 the government's own
investigators wrote that larger cores need steel wider than 932, and that the country's dependence on imported cores
threatened national security (C13, C16). Three weeks later the trade office chose a monitoring regime over a tariff
(C17). The cores kept coming in outside the steel tariff until August 2025 (C18), while the wait for a transformer
went from months to years (C34).

## The margin, in one line
The margin was the ability to build the grid's transformers from steel made at home. It rested on one mill whose widest
domain-refined coil is 920 mm and whose surface treatment does not survive the annealing that wound cores need (C07,
C08, C26), so when demand rose the margin was an import queue.

## Angles (3 considered; 1 chosen, 2 kept as devices)
1. **The 12 millimetres** (spine, chosen): one width, one temperature, one finding. The physics explains why more of
   the same steel does not close the gap; the record dates when the government was told.
2. **The five years** (Act III device): 15 OCT 2020 → 18 AUG 2025 as a calendar the film walks, page by page, while
   the import counter climbs. Strong, and politically loaded: three administrations held the file, so it is told as
   dates and documents, never as blame.
3. **The waiting list** (Act IV device): from 2 to 4 months to up to two years for a distribution transformer (C34), up
   to 60 months for a large one (C04), three heavy railcars on the continent (C05), the 2030 model (C22). The search
   angle; not the spine, because every other film already tells it.

## Fairness (full pass at narration, PLAYBOOK §9.6)
- **Cleveland-Cliffs is a company with a documented position, not a villain.** Its steel's strengths in the same
  breath as its limits: 1.01 W/kg domain-refined grade for stacked cores (C09), the $200M investment with a $75M DOE
  award (C11), about 1,200 UAW jobs (C12). Its own case on width: "The U.S. market only requires 1,400 tons of GOES in
  that width or greater" (C29), set beside Commerce's finding (C16). Never "wrong", "inferior", "defective": those are
  the think tank's words or nobody's. The 17–39% figure is said as the Breakthrough Institute's analysis (C20).
- **Howard Industries' "does not produce enough"** is a competitor's filing in a tariff docket (C30), said as an
  allegation with Cliffs' stated answer ("has sufficient capacity to meet current domestic demand", quoted in the same
  filing).
- **The 2020–2025 gap** is dates and documents only. No motive, no lobbying claim (Gemini's was rejected: no record),
  no "nobody acted" (the Mexico regime was an action). Three administrations, the same treatment: the 2020 finding and
  carve-out, the 2021 publication, the 2025 inclusion. No party names; officials by office.
- **Both policy views:** Cleveland-Cliffs applauded the 2025 tariff (C18); the Breakthrough Institute argues for
  "immediate tariff relaxation" and investment (C33). The film takes neither side.
- **No China-vs-West framing** (landscape §1); the importing countries named only as the record names them (Canada,
  Mexico, Japan, Korea).
- Terms used exactly: grain-oriented electrical steel (said once, then "transformer steel"), domain refinement, stress
  relief annealing, stacked and wound cores, Section 232 (glossed for UK viewers once: "a US law that lets the
  government restrict imports on national-security grounds").
