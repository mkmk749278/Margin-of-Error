At sixteen thousand feet, nothing on the flight deck looked wrong. The altitude held. The cabin pressure held. Every reading sat exactly where the manual said it should.

But the number that mattered was not on any screen.

It lived in a work order... a single line, opened on a factory floor and closed at the end of a shift. No signature. No inspection. No record that anyone had looked.

Engineers call it a margin of error — the gap between the load a part will carry and the load it was built to survive. Accountants have a margin too. And for years, the two were measured in the same building, by different people, with very different goals.

The N - T - S - B would spend months reading that line. This is what they found.
