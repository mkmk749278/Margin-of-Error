## The two margins (the angle so far)
- **Ship:** probable cause (p. 202): blackout "due to a loose signal wire connection to a terminal block stemming from
  the improper installation of wire-label banding". Wire 1, Terminal Block 381, HV breaker HR1, transformer TR1
  (findings 2–3, p. 199). Breakers on Manual: blackout 58 s instead of 10 s (finding 12). Flushing pump used as the
  service pump, no auto-restart, second blackout (findings 9–10); Synergy's oversight "inadequate" (finding 11).
  Main engine auto-shutdown on low cooling-water pressure met class rules but "endangered the vessel" (finding 6).
- **Bridge:** NTSB Method II calculation, Table 5 p. 135: total AF **0.002921** vs the critical/essential threshold
  **0.0001**, "almost 30 times greater". Piers 17/18 dominate (0.001430, 0.001442). MDTA "had not performed, nor was it
  required to perform" an assessment (p. 132). AASHTO guide spec 1991, 2nd ed. 2009, recommended assessing pre-1991
  bridges. **Dated record:** HSCC minutes, March 2006: MDOT said area bridges predate 1991 and "were not subject to the
  guidelines"; the MDTA representative said the Key Bridge "was not designed to withstand contact by a large vessel"
  (p. 134). 200 ft from channel edge to Pier 17/18 (p. 67). Dolphins 489 ft west of the piers, none touched.
- **Fleet still in service:** 68 bridges, 30 owners, never assessed on recent traffic (MIR-25-10; H-25-3/H-25-4).
- **Prior strike:** *Blue Nagoya*, about one-tenth Dali's displacement, stopped by Pier 17's fender. The section
  heading says 1977, the body says 1980: check against National Research Council 1983 before any use.
- **Comparator:** Delaware Memorial Bridge, eight 80 ft dolphins, about $90–100M (studies 2019), p. 71.
