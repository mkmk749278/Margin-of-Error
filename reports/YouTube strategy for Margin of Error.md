# Win the satisfied minute, not the click

Margin of Error grows fastest in late 2026 by making the film nobody else makes: the dated decision that removed a safety margin, proven from the primary record, with the physics solved, in a neutral voice. That gap is real. Every active neighbour does one or two of those four things, and none does all four. It is also the format YouTube's ranking and monetisation rules are built to reward. YouTube says it ranks on predicted viewer satisfaction ("valued watch time", trained on survey ratings), not clicks or raw minutes. Its only official A/B tool picks the winning package by watch time. Its July 2025 "inauthentic content" rule targets templated sameness across a channel, not AI voices or faceless formats (all strong). The biggest risks are the channel's own: a synthetic narrator in front of an audience that rejects anything that feels like AI deception, and a catalogue that could look like a template with the names swapped. The biggest openings are TV-set viewing, which is now YouTube's main US screen and about half of UK in-home viewing (strong), and a run of 2025–2026 final reports whose YouTube coverage was made before the report existed. Length should follow the evidence. No source supports a fixed band. The data shows a mid-length sweet spot among high performers, and also shows YouTube demoting padded ultra-long videos in 2025, so the rule should be "every minute earns its place", with the treatment justifying the run time. The final section lists concrete playbook changes. It separates the hard lines that stay (facts, fairness, licences, respect, channel separation, honesty labels) from the craft rules that should become floors rather than caps.

Strength marks follow the research notes. **Strong** means a primary platform, regulator, measurement or peer-reviewed source, or data read directly off YouTube. **Medium** means reputable secondary reporting of primary data, or a named practitioner's own words. **Weak** means vendor blogs, AI summaries or unverified claims. Any figure that goes on air still needs its own `claims.csv` row from the primary record (PLAYBOOK §9). This report is a strategy input, not a fact source for films.

## YouTube rewards satisfied minutes and punishes catalogue sameness

YouTube's public account of its recommender has stayed the same since 2021. Clicks stopped being the lead signal in 2012. Watch time is weighted by whether viewers consider it valuable, and "only videos that you rate highly with four or five stars are counted as valued watchtime". A model predicts those survey answers for every viewer (strong, [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)). The same post says news and information videos are scored by "authoritative" and "borderline" classifiers, and "any video classified borderline is demoted" (strong, [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)). Google's 2019 RecSys paper describes the ranking model behind this. It is a multi-task model that jointly predicts engagement objectives (clicks, watch time) and satisfaction objectives (likes, survey-type responses), with a separate tower that removes position bias (strong, [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/recommending-what-video-to-watch-next-a-multitask-ranking-system/)). YouTube's growth and discovery lead restated the point in September 2026: "No metric on its own is a good indicator of value", "Each viewer gets a different ranking", and the system does not "assume that all time is valuable" (strong quotes via medium reporting, [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)).

Three findings matter most for a new channel. First, **subscriptions count for less than creators think**: "even your best videos, 90% of the time, your subscribed audience isn't deciding to watch" (strong via medium, [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)). Each film has to win new viewers on its own. Second, **a channel with no history is matched on the topic the video appears to cover**, and impressions stop if no interested audience emerges (medium, paraphrase in the same report). Topic clarity in the title, thumbnail, description line 1 and spoken first minute is therefore the cold-start mechanism. The case name the viewer types has to be in all four. Third, **old videos resurface** when an event "makes this video relevant again… to a different audience" (strong via medium, [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)). A final report, a court ruling or an anniversary is a second launch for a film that already exists.

Packaging is scored the same way. Test & Compare runs up to three titles, three thumbnails, or three title-and-thumbnail pairs. The winner is "the title or combination of title and thumbnail with the highest watch time", because YouTube optimises "for overall watch time over other metrics, like click-through-rate" (strong, [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)). A thumbnail that wins clicks but loses viewers should lose its own test, so the platform's tool favours honest packaging. Hype lets fans boost videos under seven days old from channels under 500,000 subscribers, and smaller channels get the bigger boost. It reached the US and UK in August 2025 (medium, [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/08/26/youtube-hype-feature-launches-united-states-global/)). One outlet says hype does not feed recommendations at all (weak).

The monetisation policy is narrower than its press coverage. The 15 July 2025 update renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content". It disallows "image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value" and "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production". It explicitly allows the "same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different" (strong, [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)). Reviewers judge the channel as a whole: its main theme, most-viewed and newest videos, metadata and About section (strong, same page). The reused-content rule also bars "content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create" (strong, same page). YouTube's creator liaison called the change "a minor update" (strong, [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/)). Enforcement has still been real. In January 2026, 16 AI-generated channels with about 35M subscribers were terminated, and a 1.7M-subscriber faceless creator says most faceless channels in his niche "are getting demonetised" (medium, [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)). YouTube's CEO made "managing AI slop" a 2026 priority (medium, [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/21/youtube-chief-says-managing-ai-slop-is-a-priority-for-2026-.html)). In March 2026 YouTube tested a viewer pop-up asking whether a video "feels like AI slop" (medium, [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/movies/youtube-is-now-asking-viewers-if-videos-feel-like-ai-slop/)). If that answer feeds the satisfaction model, a slop impression becomes a ranking cost as well as a policy risk. That link is our inference, not something YouTube has said.

For this channel, the risk is not the narrator's voice. It is the pattern across films. If every film opens the same way, uses the same desk build, follows the same title formula and runs narration that mostly reads a report aloud over scans, a channel-level reviewer could see a template. The original analysis is what makes the work original: the mechanism solved to its threshold, the money trail joined to the margin. The policy names the evidence it looks at, so the About page and descriptions should show the method: the records used, the record ids, the corrections policy.

Disclosure rules are clear in principle and untested for our exact case. YouTube requires disclosure for "AI-generated or altered content that seems realistic". It exempts "non-realistic content" and "production assistance… script", and it lists "cloning one's own voice to create voice overs" as minor. It also states that "disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money" (strong, [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). Since May 2026 YouTube auto-labels "significant photorealistic AI" using C2PA metadata and SynthID, and shows the label below the player on long-form (medium, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/27/youtube-will-now-automatically-label-ai-videos/)). Code-rendered schematics and unaltered real documents fall outside the "realistic" trigger. A designed narrator voice that is no real person is not explicitly covered. No official ruling exists, so the Studio setting stays the owner's decision, as the repo already says.

## Length should follow the evidence, and TV makes depth pay

YouTube has never named a preferred length. The often-quoted "as long as it needs to be, but no longer" reaches us only second-hand (weak, [Toptal](https://www.toptal.com/creator/post/youtube-shorts-length)). The data that exists points two ways. Among 300,000+ high-performing 2025 videos, median views for videos under 30 minutes rose with length, peaked at **18–24 minutes**, and declined slowly after that (weak-medium, method unpublished, [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/what-actually-makes-a-youtube-video-go-viral-in-2025)). Little Dot Studios, which manages 800+ channels, saw the reverse side. After a January 2025 algorithm change, ultra-long (>120 min) viewing fell **more than 90%** from its 2024 average within three months, and the average length of suggested videos fell from 80 to 40 minutes. By 2025, average views per video had evened out across short, long and ultra-long (medium, [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/02/12/little-dot-youtube-video-length-whitepaper-viewer-habits-2026/amp/)). Read together, these say length is rewarded only while satisfaction keeps up, and YouTube steps in when length outruns it. Neither supports a fixed band, and neither shows that a 30–40 minute film that holds attention underperforms.

The competitive field shows the same thing. The biggest accident and engineering channels do well at very different lengths. Plainly Difficult cut from 25–41 minutes in 2022 to about 11 minutes and posts weekly (about 0.22M median views). Fascinating Horror holds a fixed ~11 minutes. Brick Immortar has only 53 videos, now running 1h40 to 3h15, and gets about 1.6M median views. Mentour Pilot averages ~44 minutes and Practical Engineering ~19 (all strong, scraped 06 NOV 2026, `@<handle>/videos`). Depth per minute separates these channels. Run time does not.

Two forces push toward letting a film run longer when the evidence holds. The first is the television. TV overtook mobile as the primary US screen for YouTube watch time in December 2024 (strong, [Tubefilter on the CEO letter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/02/11/neal-mohan-youtube-letter-2025-youtube-tv-watch-time-creator-studio-businesses/)). YouTube took a record **15.8% of all US TV viewing in August 2026**, about double Netflix (strong data via medium reporting, [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/10/22/nielsen-the-gauge-youtube-record-watch-time-share-us-tv/amp/)). Nielsen is changing its method from September 2026, so later months may not compare. In the UK, the TV set's share of in-home YouTube viewing rose from 34% (2023) to 41% (2024) (strong, [Ofcom Media Nations 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-adults/media-nations-2025)), and the 2026 figures imply about 46% (our arithmetic on [Ofcom Media Nations 2026](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/multi-sector/media-nations/2026/media-nations-2026-uk-report.pdf)). Australia is lower, at 11.3% of all TV-set video viewing (strong via medium, [Mediaweek](https://mediaweek.com.au/total-tv-holds-60-of-viewers-per-cent-as-youtube-grows)). YouTube's discovery lead says TV viewers want longer videos and the system responds to that demand, while "we don't want to waste your time" (strong via medium, [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)). This is demand-led, not a length bonus.

The second force is revenue. Videos of 8 minutes or more can carry mid-rolls, and automatic mid-roll slots are on by default (strong, [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6175006?hl=en)). Placing ads at natural breaks became the default from 12 May 2025, and in YouTube's test, automatic plus manual placement earned >5% more than manual alone (medium-strong, [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/02/25/youtube-is-working-to-make-ad-slots-less-interruptive)). Creators keep 55% of long-form ad revenue (strong, [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72902?hl=en-AU)). Revenue per view therefore scales with minutes actually watched. A 20-minute film watched to 50% earns more per view than a 12-minute film watched to 60%, provided impressions and click-through hold. Little Dot also reports that long-form earns a higher share from Premium, which pays by watch time (medium). Per-topic RPM figures (US ~$15–25 for finance long-form, UK ~$12–20) are vendor estimates (weak, [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-rpm-finance-niche)). The owner's earlier review already said not to choose cases by RPM, and the evidence agrees.

The conclusion is a principle, not a number. The evidence sets the length. The treatment states the target length and justifies it beat by beat. The edit cuts any minute that adds no new fact, exhibit or turn. For a case as rich as Key Bridge or Potomac, an honest film may run 25–35 minutes. For a single-decision case, 14 may be right. The five-act clock (Act III at 6:00, Act IV at 11:00) should become a set of proportions and beat duties rather than timestamps. Two timings stay fixed because the craft evidence supports them: the open loop by 60 seconds and the open question by 2:30. Act turns double as natural mid-roll points, which protects satisfaction and revenue together if the slots land in the pause, not mid-sentence. The 16–22 minute band and the act times come from the owner's spec, so the change is the owner's to approve.

## Audiences trust the document, not the messenger

Trust in news is at or near record lows in all four core markets. In 2026 it stands at **25% in the US, 30% in the UK and 37% in Canada**, with the global figure at 37%, the lowest since 2015 (strong for the US, global and Canada, [Reuters Institute DNR 2026](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary), [DNR Canada 2026](https://www.cem.ulaval.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/dnrcanada2026e.pdf); medium for the UK, [journalism.co.uk](https://www.journalism.co.uk/dnr-2026-five-key-takeaways-for-uk-news-publishers/)). American distrust splits the country in two directions. Only **8% of Republicans** trust mass media (strong, [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/695762/trust-media-new-low.aspx)), while confidence in big business is lowest among Democrats, at 11% (medium, [Gallup tabs](https://news.gallup.com/file/poll/692660/2025_07_17_Confidence%20in%20Institutions%20Topline%20and%20Tabs.pdf)). A film that scrutinises only the company reads as left, and one that scrutinises only the regulator reads as right. Some agencies are trusted almost equally by both parties: the National Weather Service at 75% favourable among Republicans and 81% among Democrats (medium, [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2025/08/27/republicans-views-of-justice-department-fbi-rebound-as-democrats-views-shift-more-negative/)). What they have in common is a reputation for being technical and non-political, which is how the channel should present safety and competence. A plurality everywhere wants news that takes no side: 45% globally against 20% who want their own view confirmed (strong, DNR 2026), and 47% against 15% in Canada (strong, DNR Canada). Avoidance is high, at 42% globally (strong). Australians are the most worried about telling real from fake online, at 77% (medium, [radioinfo](https://radioinfo.com.au/news/digital-news-report-australia-2026-public-broadcasters-the-most-trusted-interest-in-news-increases/)).

Viewers also know what they get from creators and what they miss. 65% of US news-influencer users say influencers helped them understand events (strong, [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2024/11/18/americas-news-influencers/)). Audiences rate creators as clearer and more relatable but less trustworthy and less impartial than legacy outlets (strong, DNR 2026; DNR Canada). The unmet demand is **creator clarity combined with legacy rigour**: explain how the thing works, show the primary document, take no side. Because right-leaning viewers distrust the media most, "as reported by [outlet]" is weak evidence for half the audience. The filing or report itself, with its id on screen, borrows the authority that both halves still grant to technical records.

The synthetic narrator is the channel's biggest trust liability, and the evidence shows where the penalty comes from. Across six countries including the US and UK, 12% are comfortable with fully AI-generated news. That rises to 43% when "a human leads with some AI help" and 62% for human-made news, and only 19% accept AI presenters (medium, [journalism.co.uk on Reuters 2025](https://www.journalism.co.uk/what-the-public-wants-seven-opportunities-for-newsrooms-in-the-age-of-generative-ai/)). The UK is the most sceptical market, with 6% trusting AI chatbot news answers (strong, DNR 2026). In a US radio blind test, 55% took an AI voice for human, and the human and AI reads scored within a few points. After the reveal, though, 20% viewed the AI clip less favourably, and one listener said it "feels like the station is lying" (weak, [Radio Ink](https://radioink.com/2026/07/07/radio-listeners-cant-detect-ai-voice-but-dont-trust-it-either/)). Another study found a 27% trust drop when listeners merely expected an AI voice (weak, [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/studio-resonate-ai-voice-study/)). The commercial studies agree that the penalty comes from felt deception and slop pattern-matching, not from sound quality. For a voice that already sounds good, the defence is visible human editorial work: the real exhibit, the record id, the fact-check trail, captions written by us, a correction pinned the day we learn of an error. Brick Immortar's top comments on its Key Bridge film show this audience's values exactly: "Thank you for using real subtitles… Also thanks for not using AI" and "Your diligence and care for accuracy are what keep us sticking by you" (strong, [YouTube d8o54wmXpV0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d8o54wmXpV0)). How much to say publicly about the voice is a real tension. The repo's rule is "no AI disclosure in public descriptions", and the weak-to-medium evidence says a later discovery costs more than an upfront statement. That decision belongs to the owner, and the final section flags it.

## Three camps compete, and the middle is empty

The live competition splits into camps that do not overlap. **Aviation-accident narrators** stay in the cockpit: Mentour Pilot (2.54M subscribers, ~44 min, ~1.0M median views), Captain Steeeve (1.27M, near-daily breaking news), Green Dot Aviation and Disaster Breakdown. **Business-story channels** tell the money story with thin or no engineering: ColdFusion (5.26M, ~1.0M median), Wendover (4.92M), Modern MBA, GEN, and More Perfect Union, whose framing is advocacy. **Engineering-failure channels** solve the mechanism but treat the boardroom lightly: Practical Engineering (4.84M; its Taum Sauk dam film has 11.5M views), Plainly Difficult, Fascinating Horror and Brick Immortar. All figures are strong, scraped 06 NOV 2026. Several famous names are dormant. MagnatesMedia and LEMMiNO have posted nothing for about a year and Business Casual for four (strong for the upload ages, medium for "dormant"). The field is therefore thinner than subscriber counts suggest. The official channels show demand for the record itself. USCSB's animations draw 0.4–0.7M views each with no packaging craft at all, and NTSB animations reach 0.3–0.4M, while full NTSB hearings get 7–20K (strong).

Faceless template channels pitching our exact idea get almost no views. A live search on 06 NOV 2026 for the Alaska 1282 final report turned up at least seven new 9–13 minute faceless films, all posted in the previous one to three weeks, with **7 to 260 views each**. Their titles are literal report summaries: "Four bolts nobody reinstalled: what the NTSB found inside Alaska 1282" has 13 views (strong). The repo's own scan found more zero-view channels on the same pitch (strong, `docs/research/2026-11-02_landscape.md`). The idea is already taken. What is still missing is visible quality and earned trust.

The comment sections show who the audience is. On engineering-led films, the top comments come from experts extending the mechanism ("Former composite M&P here…", 3.6K likes, on Scott Manley's Titan report film) and from viewers thanking the creator for accuracy and patience ("We are thankful for you taking your time to get all the facts", 1.3K likes) (strong, [FAAQVntpk00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FAAQVntpk00), [d8o54wmXpV0](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d8o54wmXpV0)). Ignored warnings are what move people emotionally: "Crazy how the early warning system actually worked and was just ignored" has 11K likes (strong). On advocacy films the comments become partisan fights. More Perfect Union's East Palestine film draws arguments about lobbying, Trump and Vance (strong, [fwELoxAAJWY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fwELoxAAJWY)). Advocacy framing gets views, but it would break the channel's rule of writing for both halves of the US.

The white space is specific. No active channel consistently delivers **the documented corporate or regulatory decision that removed a safety margin, proven from primary records, with the physics solved, in a neutral voice**. There is also room for a format between the 11-minute weekly narrators and the two-hour Brick Immortar epics: a film of roughly 20–35 minutes, as accurate as Brick Immortar, with the documents on screen (medium inference). By case, coverage is thinnest on Camp Fire/PG&E, where nothing notable has appeared since FRONTLINE's 1.94M-view film in 2019. East Palestine has a neutral version still to be made. Alaska 1282's final-report version is crowded only by zero-view templates (all medium). Titan is the most saturated case: Two Bit da Vinci alone has 15.9M views. Almost all of that coverage predates the Coast Guard's 335-page report and the NTSB's final report (strong for the views).

## Confirm the click, re-hook early, pay off every promise

The best-documented practitioner rule is about the first minute. The leaked MrBeast production guide, whose authenticity has been reported but not confirmed, says the title and thumbnail "set the expectations", and that a video which fails to meet them makes viewers feel "lied to" so they leave. It reports losing 21 million of about 60 million viewers in the first minute of one video (medium, [Alexander Jarvis transcription](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)). Paddy Galloway's framework, given on Colin and Samir in April 2025, has two steps: **confirm the click and establish the video in the first ~7 seconds, then introduce a new hook the thumbnail did not promise by ~30 seconds**. He also advises giving the necessary context without over-explaining, and spending 20–30% of planning time on the first minute (medium, [Rosetta transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025)). For this channel, the thumbnail's exhibit (the part, the reading, the document) should be on screen within seven seconds. The open loop the playbook already requires within 60 seconds should land by about 30 seconds, and the glosses of US and UK bodies should come after the hook. The same guide calls minutes 3–6 "the next most important part" and asks for re-engagement there (medium). The channel's Boardroom turn at about 6:00 falls on that boundary, so Act III should open on a new exhibit, never a recap. Galloway's "puzzle list" of 3–10 pieces that build toward one explanation is close to a forensic chain of causes. Long lists, he says, "feel generic" (medium).

Peer-reviewed evidence supports the channel's dislike of vague curiosity bait. In a field test of 5,288 US news headlines, "summary" headlines beat "curiosity" headlines built on questions and "Why…", by 6,701 views to 5,324 (strong, Scacco and Muddiman, *New Media & Society*, [Mapping Ignorance summary](https://mappingignorance.org/?p=6526)). A CHI 2021 study found clickbait generated no more curiosity than ordinary headlines (strong/medium, [AMiner](https://www.aminer.org/pub/60a4e58991e011f4291ce3ac/does-clickbait-actually-attract-more-clicks-three-clickbait-studies-you-must-read)), and question headlines read as less credible (weak/medium, [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/about/13722/experimenting-with-science-news-headline-format-to-maximize-engagement)). Derek Muller's line between "legitbait", which briefly and accurately conveys the content, and the deceptive "clicktrap" is the working standard. His asteroid video took off only after a simpler title and a much-changed thumbnail (medium, [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective/)). Galloway writes 30–40 title variants per video and reports a clearer, less busy thumbnail delivering about 40 times the daily views for one creator (medium). In this niche, the titles that win state a verdict on the decision about one named case, in 4–8 words: "Every Corner Cut Led to 27 Lives Lost" (626K), "Disneyland's Most Preventable Tragedy" (3.54M), "OceanGate Is Worse Than You Thought" (3.24M). The zero-view swarm uses literal "what the report found" titles (strong for the titles and views; medium for the pattern, which is confounded by those channels' low quality). A verdict word fits the channel's honesty rules when the investigator said it. The Coast Guard board called Titan's loss "preventable", and Ofgem called North Hyde "a preventable, technical fault" (strong, [USNI News](https://news.usni.org/2025/08/06/u-s-coast-guard-report-on-the-titan-submersible-implosion), [IET](https://engx.theiet.org/b/articles/posts/heathrow-blackout-traced-to-long-standing-unaddressed-substation-fault)). The verdict must still be attributed to them.

Narrative research adds one more lever. A meta-analysis of 132 effects found that narrative transportation reliably increases engagement and persuasion, and that identifiable characters and familiar topics are among its strongest drivers (strong, van Laer et al., *Journal of Consumer Research*, [City Research Online](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/16984/)). Without exploiting victims, the channel can give each film a human through-line from the record: the inspector who flagged the fault, the engineer who testified, the crew whose actions the report describes. Each is shown in their public role only, through their own documented words. Mentour Pilot's pattern of telling the sequence first and the causes second, framed around one narrative question, is the closest successful model in the niche (weak, [TheTVDB](https://thetvdb.com/series/mentour-pilot-aviation-accidents-explained/allseasons/official)).

On Shorts, recommendations have linked Shorts viewing to a channel's long-form videos since 2022 (strong, [vidIQ quoting YouTube](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-recommends-long-form-videos-shorts-viewers/)). Each Short can carry one related-video link (strong/medium, [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2023/09/01/youtube-related-links-multi-format-creators-shorts-live-long-form/)). Channels posting about five Shorts a month average 8,789 long-form views against 4,364 for channels with none, but that is correlation, not cause (medium, [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)). Galloway says to make a Short only from an idea that works in 20–30 seconds on its own (medium). The cost is low and the gain unproven, so a Short should be made only when a beat stands alone, always with the parent link. For Europe, YouTube says creators who dub at least 80% of their watch time "tend to have more success" (strong via medium, [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)). That is a later lever for the European tech hubs, and its cost and voice implications are the owner's call.

## What to change in how we make films

The hard lines stay exactly as written: facts only from primary records, fair attribution, honesty labels, licences, respect for workers and victims, channel separation, "polished only", and the owner's decisions. Nothing in the research argues for loosening them. Visible care is the channel's main competitive advantage, and an error in front of this expert audience is the most expensive failure available. The changes below fall into two groups. The first group are craft rules that should be relaxed or re-cut so they work as a floor without capping a better idea. The second group are new or tightened rules that come out of the evidence. Rules from the owner's spec are marked **(owner's decision)**: they are proposals for the owner to approve, not edits a session makes.

### Flag for the owner: synthetic-voice transparency

The repo's rule is "no AI disclosure in public descriptions", and the owner sets YouTube's "Altered or synthetic content" setting. The evidence does not overturn that rule, but it does point one way. The trust penalty for synthetic voices comes mostly from felt deception when viewers find out, not from detection by ear (weak, two commercial studies). Viewers accept "human-led with AI help" far more than fully AI-made content: 43% against 12% (medium). YouTube says its disclosure label does not limit reach (strong for the claim; untested on viewers). A one-line method note ("Narrated by the studio's synthetic voice; written, researched and checked by the studio against the records listed below") would move the channel toward the accepted band. It would also take away a hostile commenter's best line. This is the owner's decision, so it should be offered as a choice. The research behind it is too weak to support a rule change.

## Conclusion

The research changes the frame from "make films the algorithm likes" to "make films viewers rate as time well spent, and make each one visibly different from the last". YouTube's ranking model, its A/B tool and its monetisation review all point at the same target: satisfied minutes from original work. Those are also what a clinical, record-led investigation produces. The one structural threat is self-inflicted. A good pipeline that repeats its own template could set off the same channel-level review that removed the slop channels, so creativity in each film's form is now a compliance requirement as well as a craft one.

The money follows from the same choices. TV-set viewing and mid-roll economics pay for depth only while depth holds attention. So the right length rule is a justification test, not a band. The most valuable editorial asset is timing: being the first good film after a final report that YouTube's existing coverage predates. Heathrow North Hyde, Potomac and Key Bridge are ready now, and UPS 2976 needs its kit built before the NTSB publishes. Each makes the channel's thesis, that a financial margin traded away a physical one, testable on screen from the record. That, more than any packaging trick, is what the audience in these comment sections is waiting for.
