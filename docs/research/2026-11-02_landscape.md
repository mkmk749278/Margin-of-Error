# Where Margin of Error has space on YouTube, and which voice (research, 2026-11-02)

**Method.** 51 YouTube searches (YouTube Data API, long videos, English), each run for the US and the UK, on our
launch cases, the neighbouring genres (aviation disasters, engineering failures, corporate/finance investigations,
infrastructure) and UK cases. 1,045 videos from several hundred channels; the raw table is
`docs/research/2026-11-02_youtube_scan.csv`. Views are lifetime views on 2026-11-02, so older videos are favoured;
"recent" means published after mid-2024. The API's rate limit stopped 10 of the 52 search runs in round 2 (bridges,
dams, McKinsey and private-equity hospitals partly missing). Web sources for policy and voice research at the end.

---

## 1. The map: four neighbouring lanes, and the empty middle

| Lane | Who wins | Proof of demand | What they don't do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Aviation disaster** | Mayday: Air Disaster (1.1M subs, 41.5M views on our matches alone), Mentour Pilot (2.5M), Green Dot Aviation, Captain Steeeve | 15 of 30 "NTSB investigation" results over 1M; Mayday uploads still hit 2–6M in 2025 | Stay in the cockpit. The organisation, the money and the paperwork are a side note. |
| **B. Corporate / finance explainers** | Wendover ("How Boeing Lost Its Way" 2.8M, "Downfall of Southwest" 2.9M), GEN ("How Private Equity Turns…" 5.2M, 2026), More Perfect Union, CNBC, Bloomberg Originals, Veritasium ("The Biggest Chemical Coverup in History", 37.4M, 2025) | Very high, and still rising in 2025–26 | Thin engineering: the failure is told, not shown. Several are advocacy ("…And Got Away With It"). |
| **C. Engineering failure** | Practical Engineering ("Taum Sauk Dam", 11.5M), Plainly Difficult, Brick Immortar, The B1M, USCSB's own films (4.4M) | High, evergreen | Deep physics, but the boardroom is barely there; few use the documents. |
| **D. News / long-form TV** | FRONTLINE ("Boeing's Fatal Flaw" 5.3M), 60 Minutes Australia, Last Week Tonight ("Boeing" 12.3M, "Freight Trains" 7.0M) | Biggest single hits | Not built for search; one film per topic, then gone. |

**The empty middle is exactly our thesis:** the physics of the failure (lane C's depth) joined to the documented
financial decision (lane B's story), told with the record on screen (lane D's authority), in a neutral voice (unlike
the advocacy channels). In the 1,045 videos we found no channel that does all four consistently (our reading of the titles and channels, not a viewing of every film).

**But the middle is not empty of attempts.** Round 2 turned up a crowd of new faceless channels on exactly our
pitch, almost all at zero views: "The 8-K Dossier", "Vanishing Assets", "The Fragile Grid", "Red Ink Law",
"FAN FACTS", "Why Powerful Companies…". The idea alone wins nothing; template faceless work in this niche is already
common and ignored. **The edge has to be quality you can see in one second (the real exhibit, the real mechanism
moving) and trust you can feel in one minute.**

## 2. Signals that matter for us

1. **Small new channels still break out here, in 2026.** The Fool (92k subs) "OceanGate Is Worse Than You Thought",
   3.2M (May 2026); Jet Crumbs (35k) "How Boeing Strategically Killed The A380?", 1.8M (Mar 2026); Beyond Sky (40k),
   two Boeing strategy films at 0.5–0.7M; Business Explains (240k) "This Hospital Closed. The CEO Has A $40 Million
   Yacht", 0.84M. The algorithm lifts strong single films from unknown channels in this space.
2. **Boeing is the deepest well.** 17 of 32 "Boeing 737 MAX documentary" results are over 1M; the Boeing-strategy
   story keeps paying in 2025–26. Good news for Episode 1.
3. **Our exact launch cases have little definitive competition:**
   - *Alaska 1282 door plug:* one film over 1M (Mentour Pilot, 1.95M, March 2024, before the NTSB final report).
     No one has made the film from the final report and the hearing dockets. **Open.**
   - *East Palestine:* one film over 1M (More Perfect Union, 1.29M, 2025, advocacy framing); NTSB's own hearings get
     ~20k. A neutral, document-led forensic film **is open**.
   - *Power transformers / GOES:* the topic itself is small, but it rides the **AI data-centre power** wave: Business
     Insider 8.2M (2025), PBS Terra 3.7M, Bloomberg 1.45M (2026), Infographics Show 4.1M (Oct 2026). **Best timing of
     the three**, if the film is framed through the AI power demand rather than as a grid primer.
4. **Final reports are an under-used moment.** Breaking-news reactions are crowded (UPS 2976: a bodycam upload at
   26M; DCA midair: Mentour 1.1M, Captain Steeeve 0.95M), but the film made from the **final report** has far less
   competition, and our sourcing is the advantage there. A standing slot: "the final report is out; here is what it
   proves".
5. **UK cases are under-supplied but demand is unproven.** Grenfell has two films over 1M (Plainly Difficult, The
   B1M); Post Office Horizon tops out at 0.44M (ColdFusion); Thames Water, RAAC and Carillion have no long-form film
   with traction at all, only small faceless channels. Either a gap or low interest outside the UK; test with one
   UK case after the trilogy, not in it.
6. **Length.** The winners run 20–60 minutes (Mayday ~45, Mentour 40–65, FRONTLINE ~53, Wendover/GEN ~22). Our
   16–22 minute target is at the short end of the genre; let strong cases run to 25–30.
7. **Packaging that works here:** the case + a sharp human or money angle ("Disneyland's Most Preventable Tragedy",
   fern, 3.5M; "How Vail Destroyed Skiing", 3.5M; "The Real Reason The Boeing Starliner Failed", 3.3M). Titles that
   promise a reason or a hidden cause, not a list.

## 3. Our edge, in one paragraph

**The record, moving.** Others describe the documents; we put the real exhibit on screen and highlight the line,
then show the physical consequence as a true-geometry animation, with every number sourced. Clinical and neutral where
the corporate channels editorialise; organisational and financial where the aviation and engineering channels stop.
That is a credibility moat that template faceless channels cannot copy cheaply, and it is what YouTube's 2025
"inauthentic content" rule leaves alone: the rule targets mass-produced, repetitive, low-effort uploads, not AI
voices or faceless formats as such (YouTube's statement as reported; check the Help Centre wording before launch).

## 4. Recommendations

1. **Keep Episode 1 (the door plug)** as the launch: deepest demand (Boeing), no definitive film from the final
   report. Title stays honest and specific.
2. **Reframe Episode 2 through AI power demand** (working title: *AI Wants the Power. The Grid Can't Get
   Transformers.*): same mechanism and same boardroom story, but it sits in the biggest current search and browse
   wave. Fact flags in PLAYBOOK §17.3 still apply.
3. **Episode 3 (East Palestine) as the neutral counterpoint** to the advocacy films; fix the title once the train
   length is checked.
4. **Add a "final report" slot** for cases whose NTSB final report lands (the DCA midair and UPS 2976 are candidates
   when their finals publish).
5. **One UK case as film 4 or 5** (Grenfell's cladding chain, or Post Office Horizon if a mechanism angle holds), to
   test the UK appetite.
6. **Quality signals in the first second:** a real exhibit or the real part in the thumbnail, never a generic
   render; sources in the description. Upload when the film is ready, not to a schedule.

## 5. The voice

**What we could and couldn't check.** `elevenlabs.io` is blocked from this cloud session, so the voice library and
its samples could not be heard or listed here. What the sources say:
- **George** is one of ElevenLabs' default voices, described in third-party catalogues as a middle-aged British male,
  warm and refined, made for narration. That matches the spec.
- The default voices are the most-heard ElevenLabs voices on YouTube (ElevenLabs itself lists Brian, Liam, Adam and
  others as the most popular on social media). Evidence that viewers recognise and punish a default voice is only
  anecdotal; we found no study.
- Studies of AI vs human voices: listeners mostly can't tell them apart on sound alone, but **trust** is where AI
  loses, most of all in science and educational content (a TUM study on science videos; radio studies by Crowd React
  Media). For a channel whose whole edge is trust, the voice must not sound like a template.
- ElevenLabs' documentary voice pages list community British narrators (for example "Haseeb – British Documentary"
  and "Dan – warm & engaging British male"); we could not hear them from here.

**Recommendation: audition three, on the same paragraph** (`films/_voice/audition.txt`, 130 words, passes
`vo_lint.py`), all on Multilingual v2 at stability 0.62, similarity 0.80, style 0.00:
1. **A designed voice (recommended if it passes):** ElevenLabs Voice Design, prompt: *"British male, early 50s,
   neutral southern English accent, low and calm, precise diction, measured pace, dry and serious like an accident
   investigator giving evidence; no warmth in the delivery, no drama."* A voice no other channel uses removes the
   "template channel" signal at no extra cost.
2. **George** (the spec's choice): proven, warm, well suited; the risk is only that it is widely heard.
3. **A library British documentary narrator** with a high clone count and clean samples (Haseeb or Dan, or whichever
   sounds most like an investigator to you).

Judge on: does it sound like evidence or like a trailer; are "N - T - S - B" and the numbers read cleanly; does `...`
give a real pause; would you trust it for 20 minutes. Send the three MP3s to Drive `Margin-of-Error/_voice/` and we
measure pauses and pace with `scripts/vo_review.py` before you pick.
