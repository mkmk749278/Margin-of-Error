# YouTube recommendation system and monetisation/AI policy (2025-2026), for a new faceless documentary channel with a synthetic narrator

Research date: 2026-11-06. Strength tags: **[strong]** = YouTube's own statement (blog, Help Center, YouTube staff on record) or primary research; **[medium]** = reputable trade/tech press reporting with data or direct quotes; **[weak]** = vendor blog, SEO post, or unverified social claim. Dates are of the statement/article.

## 1. What YouTube has officially said about ranking signals, surfaces, testing of new videos/channels, and 2025-2026 packaging tools

### Takeaway
YouTube's own line, consistent from 2021 to 2026, is that it optimises for viewer satisfaction rather than any single metric: clicks were dropped as the lead signal in 2012, watch time is weighted by predicted satisfaction ("valued watch time", trained on survey ratings), and recommendations are "pulled" per viewer at session time rather than "pushed" to subscribers. For a new channel with no history, the system matches on the video's topic and stops serving impressions if no interested audience emerges; its official A/B tool (Test & Compare) picks winners by watch time, not CTR.

### Cited Findings
**Satisfaction, clicks, watch time, surveys**
- [strong] Cristos Goodrow (VP Engineering), 15 Sep 2021: "we learned back in 2011 that clicking on a video doesn't mean you actually watched it... That's why we added in watchtime in 2012." — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- [strong] Same post: "we measure what we call 'valued watchtime'—the time spent watching a video that you consider valuable." "Only videos that you rate highly with four or five stars are counted as valued watchtime." — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- [strong] Same post: "we've trained a machine learning model to predict potential survey responses for everyone"; they "purposely hold back some of the survey responses from the training" to check accuracy. — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- [strong] Same post: "people are more likely to be satisfied by videos that they share or like"; "If you dislike a video, that's a signal that it probably wasn't something you enjoyed watching." — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- [strong] Same post, news/information: classifiers rate videos "authoritative" or "borderline"; "The higher the score, the more the video is promoted when it comes to news and information content"; "Any video classified borderline is demoted in recommendations." — [YouTube Blog](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- [strong] How YouTube Works (undated, live 2026): the system uses "viewing behaviour" and "likes, dislikes, subscriptions and feedback, including from satisfaction surveys"; logged-in homepage shows "a mixture of personalised recommendations"; Up Next is based on "watch history, broader viewer trends and current video topics." — [How YouTube Works: Recommendations](https://www.youtube.com/howyoutubeworks/product-features/recommendations/)
- [strong] Todd Beaupré (Growth & Discovery), Creator Insider interview reported 27 Jan 2025: "we're trying to understand not just about the viewer's behavior" but how they feel; "we look at things like likes, dislikes, these survey responses"; the homepage "isn't so much about pushing it out as much as it's pulling"; time of day and device are signals; seasonality matters, look "beyond... 90 days or more." — [Search Engine Journal (medium, quoting YouTube)](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- [strong] Beaupré, same interview: creators "who dub at least 80% of the... watch time... tend to have more success" (relevant for EU markets). — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)
- [strong, via medium report] Beaupré, Creator Insider interview reported 1 Sep 2026: "No metric on its own is a good indicator of value"; "We don't want to just assume that all time is valuable"; weighting "doesn't even work such that it's the same for you at all times"; "Each viewer gets a different ranking." — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)

**Subscribers, early testing, new channels**
- [strong, via medium] Beaupré, 1 Sep 2026: on the subscribed audience, "probably all your videos have about maybe a 10% or less click-through rate"; "even your best videos, 90% of the time, your subscribed audience isn't deciding to watch"; subscribing "isn't as strong as a lot of people think." (No data published to back the 10% figure.) — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- [strong, via medium] Same: "We're not basing decisions just on those initial moments" (first hour); early reach includes people who never watched the channel. — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- [medium, paraphrase not quote] Same: a new channel with no viewer history is matched on the topic the video appears to cover; impressions stop if no interested audience emerges. YouTube tested a cap so a video is not recommended "more than eight times" to one viewer (result not given). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)
- [strong, via medium] Older videos can resurface: an event "makes this video relevant again... to a different audience than enjoyed it the first time" (Beaupré, Jan 2025). — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/how-youtubes-recommendation-system-works-in-2025/538379/)

**Length and TV**
- [strong, via medium] Beaupré, Sep 2026: viewers on TVs want longer videos and the system recommends them in response (demand-led, not a length bonus); "We don't want to waste your time." — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)

**Packaging tools**
- [strong] Help Center: Test & Compare can test up to three titles, thumbnails, or title+thumbnail pairs; the option with the highest watch time is then shown to all viewers. — [YouTube Help 16391400](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16391400?hl=en); [YouTube Help 13861714](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714?hl=en_ID)
- [weak] Title testing reported as a test in Sep 2025 and global from 4 Dec 2025; desktop Studio, long-form, up to two weeks; results reported as "watch time share" with significance required. — [thumbnailtest.com](https://thumbnailtest.com/news/youtube-releases-title-test-compare/); [monitoryt.com](https://monitoryt.com/blog/thumbnail-ab-testing); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-launches-new-thumbnail-testing-tool/). Conflict: [Descript](https://www.descript.com/blog/article/how-to-ab-test-on-youtube-for-better-video-performance) says titles can't be tested (likely outdated).
- [medium] Hype: announced 18 Sep 2024 (Made on YouTube); fans can "hype" videos under 7 days old from channels with fewer than 500,000 subscribers (Google's India blog says 500 to 500,000), up to three per week, smaller channels get a bigger boost; reached 39 markets including US and UK in Aug 2025. — [Tubefilter, 26 Aug 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/08/26/youtube-hype-feature-launches-united-states-global/); [Google blog (India)](https://blog.google/intl/en-in/products/platforms/hype-a-new-way-to-help-creators-get-discovered/); [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/youtube-expands-creator-promotion-tool-hype-to-39-markets/)
- [weak] One outlet says hyping does not affect recommendations or search (only the Hype leaderboard/promotion). — summarised in search results from [ppc.land Hype](https://ppc.land/youtubes-hype-feature-empowers-fans-to-boost-emerging-creator-visibility/); not checked against blog.youtube.

### Inferences
- Optimising for CTR alone is counter-productive: the only official A/B tool scores by watch time, and survey-weighted "valued" time is the stated target. A clinical, high-information film that viewers rate 4-5 stars is the kind of watch time the system says it values.
- News/information-style content is scored for authoritativeness; on-screen record ids and primary documents plausibly help classifier and viewer trust alike (inference, not stated).
- For a new channel, topic clarity in title/thumbnail/metadata matters because topic matching is the cold-start mechanism.
- TV viewing is a real long-form tailwind; legibility at TV distance supports the satisfaction signal.

### Gaps
- No primary statement found in this pass on upload frequency (Beaupré interview reportedly silent on it), on "key moments"/retention features, or on end-screen changes in 2025.
- Browse vs suggested vs search weighting: no official breakdown found.
- I could not open the full Creator Insider transcripts; Beaupré quotes come via SEJ and PPC Land.
- Exact Hype eligibility floor (0 vs 500 subs) unresolved.

## 2. The 15 July 2025 YPP update ("repetitious" renamed "inauthentic content"), AI voiceovers, faceless channels, and enforcement since

### Takeaway
The policy text targets template-made, mass-produced or repetitive uploads, "with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value", and is reviewed at channel level. Nothing in it bans AI voices or faceless formats, and YouTube's liaison called it a "minor update". Enforcement in 2026 has been real and channel-wide (16 large AI channels terminated in Jan 2026), and press reports that some legitimate faceless creators are being caught in it.

### Cited Findings
- [strong] YouTube announcement, 2 Jul 2025: "On July 15, 2025, YouTube is updating our guidelines to better identify mass-produced and repetitious content. This update better reflects what 'inauthentic' content looks like today." — [Search Engine Journal (quoting YouTube)](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/)
- [strong] Help Center changelog, 15 Jul 2025: policy renamed from "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content" and clarified to cover repetitive or mass-produced content; reused-content policy unchanged. — [YPP policies, YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [strong] Policy text: "content that looks like it's made with a template". Not allowed: "Image slideshows, templated storylines, or scrolling text with minimal or no narrative, commentary, or educational value"; "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production". Allowed: "Same intro and outro for your videos, but the bulk of your content is different." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [strong] Reviewers look at the channel's main theme, most viewed and newest videos, watch time, metadata and About section; "Our reused content policy applies to your channel as a whole." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [strong] Reused content not allowed: "Content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create"; clips "with little or no narrative." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)
- [strong] Rene Ritchie (Creator Liaison), early Jul 2025: "This is a minor update to YouTube's long-standing YPP policies" to "help better identify when content is mass-produced or repetitive"; 11 Jul 2025: "There are no changes to YouTube's reused content policies on clips, compilations, reaction content." — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)
- [weak] Per a vidIQ summary, Ritchie made clear YouTube is "not specifically going after... AI generated voices"; examples flagged were story channels with superficial differences between uploads and slideshow channels with the same narration. — [rosetta.to (vidIQ summary)](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/is-this-actually-true)
- [medium] Jan 2026: YouTube terminated 16 AI-generated channels (about 35M subscribers, 4.7B lifetime views, est. ~$10M/yr) under the inauthentic content policy; Kapwing-based reports say 11 deleted, 5 emptied. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage); [OutlierKit (weak)](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-2026/)
- [medium] A 1.7M-subscriber faceless creator told The Hollywood Reporter that most faceless channels in his niche "are getting demonetised"; enforcement is channel-level, so a pattern across recent uploads can hit every video. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- [medium] Mar 2026: YouTube reportedly tested a mobile pop-up asking viewers to rate whether a video feels like AI slop (five-point scale). — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- [weak] Claims of "thousands" of demonetised channels and that the algorithm now favours on-camera faces (Jun 2026) appear only in vendor blogs. — [milx.app](https://milx.app/en/news/why-youtube-just-suspended-thousands-of-ai-channels-and-how-to-protect-yours); [scalelab](https://scalelab.com/en/why-youtube-is-cracking-down-on-ai-generated-content-in-2026)

### Inferences
- The risk factors are templating, sameness across uploads, and "readings of other materials" without our own narrative. A film-per-case investigation with original scripting, original analysis and bespoke visuals sits on the allowed side, but the channel-level review means each film must look visibly distinct (different exhibits, structure, thumbnail), not one template with names swapped.
- Narration that only reads a report aloud over scans could be read as "readings of other materials"; the original analysis (mechanism solve, money trail) is what makes it original.
- Faceless channels face a higher false-positive risk at review; an About page and descriptions that show method (sources, record ids, corrections) are what reviewers are told to look at.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube count of channels demonetised under the renamed policy.
- No official written statement found on AI voiceovers specifically for YPP; only liaison videos via summaries.
- Appeal outcomes for wrongly demonetised faceless channels are not reported.

## 3. Altered or synthetic content disclosure: when required, when not, and effect on reach

### Takeaway
Disclosure is required only for realistic AI content that could be mistaken for real people, places or events. Production assistance (scripts, outlines) is exempt, and so is using AI that is not realistic. YouTube states repeatedly (Help Center; May 2026 announcement) that the label does not limit reach or monetisation. Since May 2026 YouTube also auto-labels "significant photorealistic AI" and shows the label below the player on long-form.

### Cited Findings
- [strong] "We require creators to disclose AI-generated or altered content that seems realistic", including making a real person appear to say or do something they didn't. — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- [strong] Not required: "non-realistic content that's made with AI"; minor edits; "Production assistance, like using generative AI tools to create or improve a video outline, script"; listed as minor: "Cloning one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs". — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- [strong] "Disclosing AI content alone won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money." Repeated failure to disclose can lead to labels being added, removal or YPP suspension. — [YouTube Help 14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)
- [medium, quoting YouTube] 27 May 2026: YouTube auto-labels when internal systems detect "significant photorealistic AI", using C2PA metadata and SynthID; fully generative C2PA content gets permanent labels; labels now sit directly below the player on long-form (overlaid on Shorts); YouTube says labels don't affect recommendation or monetisation; creators can correct misidentified disclosure. — [TechCrunch, 27 May 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/27/youtube-will-now-automatically-label-ai-videos/)
- [medium] Unrealistic or animated AI content gets a label only in the expanded description. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/27/youtube-will-now-automatically-label-ai-videos/)

### Inferences
- A synthetic narrator voice that is not a real person and reads our own script, over code-rendered (non-photoreal) graphics and real unaltered documents, appears to fall outside the "realistic" trigger. The Help page's voice example covers cloning *one's own* voice, so a stock or designed voice is not explicitly listed; the safe course is consistent with the repo's rule that the owner decides the Studio flag.
- Photoreal reconstructions of real events (e.g., an AI-rendered aircraft or plant) would trigger disclosure and auto-labelling; code-rendered schematics would not.
- Per YouTube, ticking the box carries no reach penalty; viewer perception of the more prominent 2026 label is untested.

### Gaps
- The Help page shows no "last updated" date.
- No independent data on whether labelled videos get lower CTR or satisfaction.
- No explicit official ruling on a non-cloned synthetic narrator voice for factual narration.

## 4. YouTube statements on AI "slop", likeness detection, and how original faceless channels stay eligible

### Takeaway
YouTube's CEO made "managing AI slop" a 2026 priority while saying AI is a tool, "not a replacement". Likeness detection widened through 2026 to politicians, journalists, celebrities and then any adult. Eligibility for AI-assisted channels rests on originality and added value, not on showing a face.

### Cited Findings
- [medium, quoting CEO letter] Neal Mohan, annual letter 21 Jan 2026: managing AI slop and detecting deepfakes are priorities; "It's becoming harder to detect what's real and what's AI-generated"; AI as a tool, "not a replacement"; over 1M channels used YouTube's AI creation tools daily in Dec 2025; plans to extend Content ID-style tools to manage likeness in AI content. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/21/youtube-chief-says-managing-ai-slop-is-a-priority-for-2026-.html); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/digital/youtube-ceo-neal-mohan-2026-letter-ai-slop-1236479185/); [Variety AU](https://au.variety.com/2026/digital/news/youtube-channels-using-ai-tools-reduce-slop-neal-mohan-letter-32190/)
- [medium] Likeness/deepfake detection expanded to politicians, government officials and journalists (10 Mar 2026), celebrities (21 Apr 2026), then any adult. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/27/youtube-will-now-automatically-label-ai-videos/)
- [strong] Policy allows a recurring intro/outro and series formats where "the bulk of your content is different"; reused material is fine with "significant original commentary, modifications" (Ritchie, 11 Jul 2025). — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-targeting-spam-not-reaction-channels/550755/)
- [medium] Apr 2026: an open letter from 230 experts asked YouTube to bar AI content from YouTube Kids (context for policy direction). — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)

### Inferences
- Likeness detection concerns faces (and, per plans, broader likeness). A channel that never synthesises a real person's face or voice stays clear of it.
- The "slop" crackdown and viewer slop surveys make visible craft and evident human editorial judgment more valuable: a "made-by-AI" first impression (stock AI imagery, generic TTS cadence) is a risk to both survey satisfaction and review.

### Gaps
- No primary YouTube blog post retrieved for the Jan 2026 letter (press only), and none for the slop-rating pop-up.
- No official guidance found on voice likeness detection (audio).

## 5. How the satisfaction model and multi-objective ranking work in Google/YouTube research

### Takeaway
Google's 2019 RecSys paper describes YouTube's ranking as a multi-task model, Multi-gate Mixture-of-Experts (MMoE), that jointly predicts engagement objectives (clicks, watch time) and satisfaction objectives (likes, survey-type responses), with a shallow tower to remove position/selection bias. The 2021 blog post confirms that survey-predicted "valued watch time" is in production. I found no 2024-2026 paper detailing changes.

### Cited Findings
- [strong] Zhao et al., "Recommending What Video to Watch Next: A Multitask Ranking System", RecSys 2019: a "large scale multi-objective ranking system", built on Wide & Deep, adopts MMoE "to automatically learn parameters to share across potentially conflicting objectives", and adds "a shallow tower to model and remove selection bias"; reports "substantial improvements on recommendation quality." — [Google Research](https://research.google/pubs/recommending-what-video-to-watch-next-a-multitask-ranking-system/)
- [strong] Production confirmation: survey ratings (4-5 stars) define valued watch time; a model predicts survey answers for all viewers. — [YouTube Blog, 15 Sep 2021](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/on-youtubes-recommendation-system/)
- [strong, via medium] 2026 restatement that the weighting differs by viewer and context ("doesn't even work such that it's the same for you at all times"). — [PPC Land, 1 Sep 2026](https://ppc.land/subscribers-skip-90-of-uploads-in-their-feed-youtube-director-says/)

### Inferences
- Position-bias correction means a video shown low on the page isn't punished for that; what matters is engagement and satisfaction relative to placement.
- Likes, shares and high survey ratings are trained objectives, so a calm, trustworthy delivery that leaves viewers feeling the time was well spent is, in principle, what gets rewarded.

### Gaps
- The full 2019 paper text (exact objectives list) couldn't be fetched (ACM 403); the engagement/satisfaction split is from the abstract and well-known summaries, not re-verified line by line.
- No published 2024-2026 YouTube ranking paper was found in this pass.
