# Audience mentality: US, UK, Canadian and Australian viewers of documentary, investigative and explainer content (2024–2026)

Researched 2026-11-06. Strength marks: **[strong]** = primary source read directly; **[medium]** = reputable secondary coverage of a named primary study, or primary with partial read; **[weak]** = industry/commercial study, unverified methods, or single low-profile source. Most numbers below need checking against the primary page before any on-air or public use.

## 1. Trust in news and institutions, by country

### Takeaway
Trust in news is at or near record lows across all four markets in 2026 (US 25%, UK 30%, Canada 37%; global 37%), and US distrust is sharply partisan (Republicans 8% trust mass media per Gallup). Public broadcasters (BBC, SBS/ABC, CBC) remain the most trusted brands, and business is now trusted more than media or government.

### Cited Findings
- **[strong]** Reuters Institute DNR 2026 (published 16 June 2026; 97,520 respondents, ~2,000 per market, 48 markets): global trust in news 37%, lowest since measurement began in 2015 (−3 pp); trust fell significantly in 29 of 48 markets. US trust 25% (−5 pp); right-leaning Americans 15%. — [DNR 2026 executive summary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary)
- **[medium]** UK (DNR 2026): trust in news 30% (−5 pp). Most trusted brands BBC 59%, ITV 57%, Channel 4 57%. — [journalism.co.uk, DNR 2026 UK takeaways](https://www.journalism.co.uk/dnr-2026-five-key-takeaways-for-uk-news-publishers/)
- **[strong]** Canada (DNR 2026 Canada report, Centre d'études sur les médias, Université Laval): trust in "most news most of the time" 37% (−2 pp), lowest yet; Francophones 44%, Anglophones 35%. 40% say CBC/Radio-Canada news has a positive effect on Canadian life, 19% negative; on the political left 63% positive; on the right 42% negative vs 31% positive. Right-leaning respondents view coverage negatively across all six topics tested. — [DNR Canada 2026 PDF](https://www.cem.ulaval.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/dnrcanada2026e.pdf)
- **[medium]** Australia (DNR Australia 2026, University of Canberra, 16 June 2026): public broadcasters most trusted, then local/regional newspapers; SBS reported equal-first most trusted brand; 49% see public service media as positive; interest in news up to 57% since 2023; 77% worry about telling real from fake online, reported as the highest globally. Overall trust % not found. — [radioinfo.com.au](https://radioinfo.com.au/news/digital-news-report-australia-2026-public-broadcasters-the-most-trusted-interest-in-news-increases/); [University of Canberra newsroom](https://www.canberra.edu.au/about-uc/media/newsroom/2026/june/dnr-2026-younger-australians-are-rewriting-the-rules-of-news-consumption); [SBS media release](https://www.sbs.com.au/aboutus/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Media-Release-Australians-place-growing-trust-in-SBS-News-16-June-2026.pdf)
- **[medium]** DNR 2025 (June 2025): global trust ~40%, stable three years; nearly 60% worry they can't tell what is true online. — [DNR 2025 PDF](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2025-06/Digital_News-Report_2025.pdf) (figures via secondary summary [What's New in Publishing](https://whatsnewinpublishing.substack.com/p/reuters-institute-2025-digital-news))
- **[strong]** Gallup (poll 2–16 Sept 2025): 28% of Americans trust mass media a "great deal"/"fair amount", first time under 30%. Republicans 8% (first single-digit reading), independents 27%, Democrats 51% (record low for Democrats; −19 pp over three years). 2024 reading was 31%. — [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/695762/trust-media-new-low.aspx); [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2025/10/02/media-trust-new-low)
- **[medium]** Gallup Confidence in Institutions (June 2025): 15% have a great deal/quite a lot of confidence in big business, among the lowest since 1973; Republicans 26%, independents 13%, Democrats 11%. Party gap on the composite measure is the largest in 46 years. — [Gallup tabs PDF](https://news.gallup.com/file/poll/692660/2025_07_17_Confidence%20in%20Institutions%20Topline%20and%20Tabs.pdf); [GV Wire](https://gvwire.com/2025/07/22/americans-confidence-in-institutions-remains-low-divides-by-party-widen/)
- **[medium]** Edelman Trust Barometer 2026 (33,938 respondents, 28 countries): "grievance has devolved into insularity"; ~70% hesitant or unwilling to trust people with different values, information sources or backgrounds; business rated more ethical and competent than other institutions. UK: 76% hesitant to trust those with differing values (secondary report). — [Edelman press release](https://www.edelman.com/news-awards/2026-edelman-trust-barometer-society-slides-into-insularity); [GB News (UK figure)](https://www.gbnews.com/news/britons-dont-trust-people-with-different-values)
- **[medium]** Ofcom (April 2026): 19% of UK adults always trust mainstream news to be accurate; 21% always question its accuracy. — [Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/04/02/ofcom-trust-in-news-sources-mixed-social-media-usage-down/)

### Inferences
- In all four markets the trusted reference points are public-service broadcasters and official/primary sources; a channel that shows the record itself (docket ids, report pages) borrows that trust rather than asking for its own.
- Low trust in both media and big business means a corporate-accountability film can't rely on "the press says" or "the company says"; it must show the document.

### Gaps
- DNR 2026 overall trust % for Australia, and US/UK/CA/AU country numbers for Edelman 2026, were not retrieved. DNR 2025 per-country figures not verified.

## 2. Who watches news/explainers on YouTube; news influencers and what audiences value

### Takeaway
Online video is now the main news route in most markets (77% weekly news video globally; YouTube 34% for news), and creators reach 27% (news-focused) to 46% (any type). Audiences find creators clearer and more relatable but less trustworthy and impartial than legacy outlets: the gap a primary-source, impartial independent can fill.

### Cited Findings
- **[strong]** DNR 2026: 77% watch online news video weekly; YouTube used for news by 34% (69% use it at all); online video news passes broadcast TV news in 45 markets; publishers' own video share fell to 23% (−10 pp since 2021); 27% watch on-demand news via YouTube-type apps on smart TVs. — [DNR 2026 executive summary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary)
- **[strong]** DNR 2026: 27% get news from news-focused creators, 46% from creators of any type; among news-creator audiences 47% say most/all news needs are met (13% of all respondents); 3% rely solely on creators. Creators rated more entertaining and relatable but less trustworthy and less impartial than traditional outlets. — [DNR 2026 executive summary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary)
- **[strong]** Canada 2026: 38% consumed news from creators/influencers in the past week (Anglophones 43%, Francophones 27%); creators seen as more entertaining, easier to understand, more relatable and up to date; traditional brands as more authentic, knowledgeable, trustworthy and impartial. 27%+ of older Canadians use YouTube-type apps on smart TVs for news (figure list). — [DNR Canada 2026](https://www.cem.ulaval.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/dnrcanada2026e.pdf)
- **[strong]** Pew-Knight "America's News Influencers" (18 Nov 2024; survey of 10,658 US adults, 15 Jul–4 Aug 2024; 500 influencers sampled across five platforms): 21% of US adults and 37% of 18–29s regularly get news from news influencers, with minimal Republican/Democrat difference. 77% of influencers have no news-organisation affiliation; 27% explicitly right-leaning, 21% left-leaning, about half no clear lean. Among users, 65% say influencers helped them better understand current events; 70% say the news is at least somewhat different from other sources; 31% feel a personal connection. — [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2024/11/18/americas-news-influencers/)
- **[medium]** Pew 2024: YouTube news influencers more likely to identify with the right than the left; few have news-industry links; 49% also host a podcast. — [Pew, news influencers on YouTube](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2024/11/18/news-influencers-on-youtube/)
- **[medium]** Ofcom News Consumption in the UK 2025 (21 Jul 2025): online news 70% of adults vs TV 68%; among 16–24s the top news access points were TikTok 31%, YouTube 30%, Instagram 24%; 16–24 trust in social-media news rose from 45% to 52%; traditional providers still rated higher for trust, accuracy and impartiality. Ofcom 2026 (15 Sep 2026): Facebook 39% vs YouTube 33% of adults encountering news in past month, YouTube closing the gap. — [MediaCat](https://mediacat.uk/uk-adults-spend-61-minutes-per-day-consuming-news/); [Advanced Television](https://www.advanced-television.com/2026/09/15/ofcom-news-content-increasingly-curated-by-online-gatekeepers/); [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/attitudes-to-news/one-in-five-brits-using-ai-to-keep-up-with-news)

### Inferences
- The smart-TV figure (27% globally) backs the repo's "TV across the room" design rule.
- Viewers prize creators for clarity ("helped me understand"); they mark them down on trust and impartiality. A channel that keeps creator clarity but adds legacy-grade sourcing and impartiality targets the exact gap in the DNR/Canadian comparison.

### Gaps
- No survey found that isolates long-form documentary/explainer viewers (as opposed to "news") by age on YouTube. No quantitative comment-section analysis of top documentary channels was found.

## 3. Attitudes to AI in content and AI voices

### Takeaway
Audiences are broadly uncomfortable with AI-made news (about 12% comfortable with fully AI-generated news vs 62% with human-made), most accepting when humans lead and AI does back-end tasks, and least accepting of AI presenters (19%). Listeners often can't detect a good synthetic voice, but trust drops when they learn or suspect it is AI: disclosure and perceived deception, not sound quality, drive the penalty.

### Cited Findings
- **[medium]** Reuters Institute "Generative AI and News Report 2025" (YouGov, ~2,000 per country in Argentina, Denmark, France, Japan, UK, US; 5 Jun–15 Jul 2025): 12% comfortable with fully AI-generated news; 21% with AI and a "human in the loop"; 43% when a human leads with some AI help; 62% for entirely human-made news. Most-accepted uses: spelling/grammar 55%, translation 53%; AI-generated presenters/authors 19%. — [journalism.co.uk summary](https://www.journalism.co.uk/what-the-public-wants-seven-opportunities-for-newsrooms-in-the-age-of-generative-ai/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/409717/leaning-into-ai-people-trust-its-use-in-news-but.html); [report record](https://users.ox.ac.uk/~polf0572/publication/generative-ai-and-news-report-2025/)
- **[strong]** DNR 2026: weekly AI-chatbot news use 10% (under-35s 16%); trust in AI chatbot answers 20% globally, UK 6%; UK chatbot news use 4%, lowest of any market. — [DNR 2026 executive summary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary); [journalism.co.uk](https://www.journalism.co.uk/dnr-2026-five-key-takeaways-for-uk-news-publishers/)
- **[medium]** Australia 2026: ~9% use AI chatbots for news; trust in AI-platform news very low. Canada 2026: 8% use chatbots for news (+2 pp). — [radioinfo.com.au](https://radioinfo.com.au/news/digital-news-report-australia-2026-public-broadcasters-the-most-trusted-interest-in-news-increases/); [DNR Canada 2026](https://www.cem.ulaval.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/dnrcanada2026e.pdf)
- **[medium]** Ofcom (Sep 2026): one in five UK adults use AI tools such as ChatGPT to keep up with news. — [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/attitudes-to-news/one-in-five-brits-using-ai-to-keep-up-with-news)
- **[medium]** Pew (April 2025, secondary): about one in ten Americans think AI will improve news and elections; nearly two-thirds expect job losses (vs 39% of experts). — [Finger Lakes 1 (AP)](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2025/04/03/ai-experts-are-optimistic-but-americans-are-wary-pew-survey-shows/)
- **[weak]** Crowd React Media blind test (May–June 2026; 1,326 US weekly radio listeners): 55% of the AI-voice group thought the AI voice was human; human and AI reads scored within a few points on credibility, authenticity, likability (overall appeal 60% vs 61%). Hypothetically, 33% would view a station less favourably if it used AI voices. After the reveal: human clip 48% more favourable / 4% less; AI clip 25% more / 20% less. A negative listener: "It feels like the station is lying to listeners." — [Radio Ink, 7 Jul 2026](https://radioink.com/2026/07/07/radio-listeners-cant-detect-ai-voice-but-dont-trust-it-either/)
- **[weak]** Studio Resonate (SiriusXM ad unit; ~1,300 US respondents): trust dropped 27% when listeners were primed to expect an AI voice, and human voices labelled AI were penalised just as much. — [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/studio-resonate-ai-voice-study/)
- **[weak]** iHeartMedia "Guaranteed Human" policy cites internal research that 90% of consumers want media made by real humans. — [Radio Ink](https://radioink.com/2026/07/07/radio-listeners-cant-detect-ai-voice-but-dont-trust-it-either/)
- **[medium]** "AI slop" backlash: from March 2026 YouTube tested a post-view prompt asking whether a video "feels like AI slop" (no results published). Kapwing found 21% of the first 500 Shorts served to a new account were AI slop. Reports from June 2026 say YouTube's crackdown is hitting faceless human creators too. — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/movies/youtube-is-now-asking-viewers-if-videos-feel-like-ai-slop/); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/110735-over-21-youtube-now-ai-slop-report.html); [The Next Web, 15 Jun 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)

### Inferences
- For a faceless channel with a synthetic narrator, the risk is less "detection by ear" and more "felt deception" and slop pattern-matching (generic visuals, templated scripts). Visible human editorial work (real exhibits, record ids, corrections, a fact-check trail) is what moves content from the 12% band toward the 43% "human-led with AI help" band.
- The UK is the most AI-sceptical of the four (6% trust in chatbot news).
- YouTube's slop survey means "feels like AI slop" is likely a ranking signal; distinctive, evidence-dense visuals matter commercially, not only ethically.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found specifically on documentary/explainer narration by synthetic voice; voice studies above are commercial (radio/ads). Labelling-preference figures (e.g., share wanting AI labels) from Reuters 2025 not verified. No YouGov AI-voice poll retrieved.

## 4. Fatigue with outrage, clickbait and partisanship; news avoidance; appetite for impartial, calm content

### Takeaway
Selective news avoidance is high and stable (42% globally; 70% of Canadians at least occasionally), and clear pluralities in every market prefer news that doesn't take sides (about 45–48% globally, 47% in Canada, vs ~15–20% who want news that shares their view).

### Cited Findings
- **[strong]** DNR 2026: 42% sometimes or often avoid news (29% in 2017). 45% prefer news that does not take sides vs 20% news that shares their view (body text says 48%; figures conflict within the report page); 46% think non-partisan news is best for society; 62% concerned about what is real/fake online (+4 pp). — [DNR 2026 executive summary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary)
- **[strong]** Canada 2026: 70% (Anglophones 71%) actively reduced news access at least occasionally; 47% prefer sources without a particular point of view vs 15% that share theirs and 14% that challenge it, with little difference by age. — [DNR Canada 2026](https://www.cem.ulaval.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/dnrcanada2026e.pdf)
- **[medium]** Australia 2026: 77% worried about telling real from fake online. — [radioinfo.com.au](https://radioinfo.com.au/news/digital-news-report-australia-2026-public-broadcasters-the-most-trusted-interest-in-news-increases/)

### Inferences
- The avoidance literature (DNR, prior years) attributes avoidance to negativity and feeling worn out; a calm, explanatory register is the counter-offer. This is inference only; the 2026 reasons breakdown was not retrieved.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 survey found that measures fatigue with YouTube clickbait/thumbnails specifically. DNR 2026 reasons-for-avoidance breakdown not retrieved.

## 5. US partisan sensitivity: regulators, corporations, "mainstream media"; framing that reaches both halves

### Takeaway
Distrust of media is bipartisan but deepest on the right (Republicans 8%); distrust of big business is deepest on the left (Democrats 11%); views of federal agencies swing with who holds the White House. Agencies that read as technical and non-political (National Weather Service, National Park Service) are rated favourably by about three-quarters of both parties: a precedent for framing safety and competence as non-partisan.

### Cited Findings
- **[strong]** Gallup Sept 2025: media trust Republicans 8%, independents 27%, Democrats 51%. — [Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/695762/trust-media-new-low.aspx)
- **[medium]** Gallup June 2025: confidence in big business Republicans 26%, independents 13%, Democrats 11%. — [Gallup tabs](https://news.gallup.com/file/poll/692660/2025_07_17_Confidence%20in%20Institutions%20Topline%20and%20Tabs.pdf)
- **[medium]** Pew agency favourability (3,554 US adults, 4–10 Aug 2025): 13 of 16 agencies rated more favourably than not. Republicans' views of DOJ and HHS rose sharply (HHS 33%→51%) as Democrats' fell (HHS 68%→47%; DOJ −27 pp to 28%). National Park Service favourable 78% R / 79% D; National Weather Service 75% R / 81% D. FAA, NTSB etc. not found in results. — [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2025/08/27/republicans-views-of-justice-department-fbi-rebound-as-democrats-views-shift-more-negative/)
- **[strong]** DNR 2026: right-leaning Americans' trust in news 15% vs 25% overall. — [DNR 2026 executive summary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary)
- **[strong]** Canada 2026: right-leaning respondents negative about coverage on every topic; left negative only on climate. — [DNR Canada 2026](https://www.cem.ulaval.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/dnrcanada2026e.pdf)

### Inferences
- A film that scrutinises the company only reads as "left"; one that scrutinises only the regulator reads as "right". Holding both to the same standard of record, and naming the mechanism (engineering, competence, safety margins) rather than ideology, matches the profile of the bipartisan-trusted technical agencies.
- Because views of agencies swing with the administration, avoid framing a regulator's failing as a current-administration story; date it and tie it to the record.
- Since the right distrusts media most, avoid "as reported by [outlet]" as evidence; cite the primary filing or report directly.

### Gaps
- No direct survey found on cross-partisan reception of corporate-accountability documentaries or on FAA/NTSB favourability by party in 2025–2026.

## 6. What viewers say they want more of

### Takeaway
Survey evidence supports wanting understanding (65% of influencer-news users value "helped me understand"), impartiality (plurality preference for no-sides news) and authenticity/knowledge (where traditional outlets still beat creators). No rigorous comment-section analysis was found.

### Cited Findings
- **[strong]** Pew 2024: 65% say influencers helped them better understand events; 70% value that it differs from other sources. — [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/journalism/2024/11/18/americas-news-influencers/)
- **[strong]** DNR 2026: most-used chatbot news feature is asking follow-up questions (42%), suggesting appetite for explanation in depth; 42% of chatbot news users always/often click through to original sources. — [DNR 2026 executive summary](https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/dnr-executive-summary)
- **[strong]** Canada 2026: traditional brands seen as more authentic, knowledgeable, trustworthy, impartial; creators as easier to understand, more entertaining. — [DNR Canada 2026](https://www.cem.ulaval.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/dnrcanada2026e.pdf)

### Inferences
- The demand profile is "creator clarity + legacy rigour": explain how the thing works, show the primary document, stay impartial.

### Gaps
- No published comment-section analysis of top documentary channels (e.g., engineering-failure or investigative explainers) found in 2024–2026; a manual review of comments would be needed. Knight Foundation 2024–2026 work not retrieved.
