# Storytelling and packaging craft for long-form documentary and investigative YouTube (2023–2026)

Research date: 06 NOV 2026. Strength ratings: **strong** = primary source or official platform documentation, or peer-reviewed
evidence; **medium** = a named practitioner's own words, reported second-hand, or a single study; **weak** = an
aggregator, AI summary or anonymous claim. "Evidence" means data or a test; "opinion" means a practitioner's view.
Note: several sources found were AI-generated summaries of videos (Recapio, Podwise, Rosetta transcripts). They are
marked as such; the original videos should be checked before quoting anyone word for word on air or in the playbook.

## Cold opens and the first 30–60 seconds

### Takeaway
The best-documented rule from 2024–2025 practitioners is: confirm the click within seconds (show that the video is what the
title and thumbnail promised), then add a new hook the thumbnail did not promise by about 30 s, and plan the first minute
far more heavily than any other minute because that is where the largest drop happens.

### Cited Findings
- MrBeast production guide (leaked Aug–Sep 2024; 36 pages; "How to Succeed in MrBeast Production"): the first minute is
  where most viewers leave; quote: "On this particular video we lost 21 million viewers in the first minute of the video"
  (of about 60 million clicks, framed as a reasonably good result). **Medium** (evidence from their own analytics, but the
  document's authenticity is reported, not confirmed by the company: two former producers vouched for it per Passionfruit;
  Creator Handbook says it was not confirmed) — [Alexander Jarvis transcription](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/); [Passionfruit](https://passionfru.it/mrbeast-leaked-document-81151/); [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/leaked-document-allegedly-reveals-mrbeasts-secrets-to-youtube-success-the-key-takeaways/)
- Same guide: the title and thumbnail "set the expectations for the viewer for your video"; if the video doesn't deliver
  them, "You'd feel like you were lied to and click off." Producers must know the title and thumbnail before they start.
  **Medium** (opinion) — [Alexander Jarvis transcription](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Gigazine's summary of the same guide: production "starts with a title and thumbnail"; the first minute shows the
  thumbnail is met; an "element of surprise" early (example: a house lowered by crane about 30 s into a video).
  **Medium** — [Gigazine, 17 SEP 2024](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240917-mrbeast-youtube-production)
- Tubefilter (17 SEP 2024): the document centres on CTR, average view duration (AVD) and average view percentage (AVP);
  the reporter "cleaned up" quote errors, so wording may differ from the original. **Medium** — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/09/17/mrbeast-internal-production-guide-leaked-key-points/amp/)
- Paddy Galloway (strategist for MrBeast and others) on Colin and Samir, "The New Rules of YouTube (2025)", 09 APR 2025:
  (a) title, thumbnail and core idea are the biggest levers; a better intro alone rarely makes a dramatic jump in views;
  (b) for a 10-minute video his clients may spend 20–30% of planning on the first minute; (c) Donaldson judged an edit by
  its first 30 s, because a weak start "anchors" the viewer negatively and reduces leeway for the rest; (d) intro
  formula: deliver on the title/thumbnail promise at once, "make them feel like they made the right decision", give the
  necessary context without over-explaining, flow straight into the first storyline; (e) his team's brand framework:
  confirm the click and establish the video in the first ~7 s, introduce a new hook the thumbnail did not promise by
  ~30 s; viewers who stay to the end get their loops closed; (f) open with several questions and promise the answer.
  **Medium** (opinion of a top practitioner; auto-generated transcript with unlabelled speakers) — [Rosetta transcript of Colin and Samir](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025)
- Galloway in other interviews (Jon Youshaei; Jay Clouse) spends heavy effort on the first lines; "a slightly better title
  or thumbnail can dramatically increase views". **Weak** (AI summaries) — [Recapio, Youshaei interview](https://recapio.com/digest/meet-mrbeast-s-secret-youtube-consultant-paddy-galloway-interview-by-jon-youshaei); [Recapio, Clouse interview](https://recapio.com/digest/meet-the-man-who-solved-youtube-paddy-galloway-interview-by-jay-clouse)
- Johnny Harris (How I Write podcast, 12 MAR 2025): segments on "Crafting Engaging First Lines and Setting Emotional
  Tone", and "The Promise and Payoff"; a clear promise in the title and thumbnail, then broaden curiosity beyond the
  initial hook. **Weak** (Podwise outline, almost no direct quotes) — [Podwise](https://podwise.ai/episodes/3299864)

### Inferences
- For Margin of Error, Act I's in-media-res open on the record already fits "confirm the click": the first ~7 s should show
  the exact object of the thumbnail (the part, the reading, the document) so the viewer knows the promise is being kept.
- The "new hook by 30 s" maps naturally onto the named open loop the channel already requires in the first 60 s; it could
  be tightened to land by ~30 s (e.g. the dated document that will be opened in Act III).
- Over-explaining context in the cold open is the main risk for a forensic channel; the glossing of US/UK bodies should be
  deferred until after the hook where possible.

### Gaps
- No primary MrBeast PDF could be retrieved (the hosted copy returned 404); quotes rely on a transcription site.
- No 2025–2026 YouTube official statement on intro length or "first 30 seconds" was found.

## Open loops, mid-video re-hooks and act structure

### Takeaway
Practitioner guidance (not tested evidence) puts the next most important stretch at minutes 3–6, uses "re-engagements"
around minutes 3 and 6, and recommends that every loop opened in the intro is closed by the end; Galloway's "puzzle list"
(each piece builds toward a complete explanation) is the closest analogue to a forensic chain of causes.

### Cited Findings
- MrBeast guide: minutes 1–3 move from hype to the story; minutes 3–6 are "the next most important part of the video";
  "It's important to re-engage the viewer around this time because they could get bored of the story and click off";
  "Typically the not as good content would be in the back half of the video" because invested viewers forgive lulls;
  "crazy progression" compresses several days into the opening minutes. **Medium** (opinion from analytics; authenticity
  reported) — [Alexander Jarvis transcription](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- Gigazine's reading: "particularly interesting content at 3 and 6 minutes" as re-engagement. **Medium** — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240917-mrbeast-youtube-production)
- Galloway (Colin and Samir, 2025): "puzzle list" – each item is a piece that builds toward a complete explanation;
  3–10 items feel like a puzzle, long lists feel generic; videos should be "familiar but unexpected"; write an
  expected-outcome prediction before upload to judge performance; "respect their time" once the click is earned (Samir's
  rule). **Medium** — [Rosetta transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025)
- vidIQ episode with a strategist breaks down an 18-million-view Red Bull video with a retention framework adapted from a
  five-act structure (hook, rising action, climax). **Weak** (search summary only) — [vidIQ on Rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/youtube-expert-reveals-his-viral-video-formula?t=396)
- Johnny Harris (2025): writes script and visual direction at the same time, with colour-coded notes for visual assets and
  fact-checks; pillars "Visual, Plain, Clear, Concrete"; "Active Prose and Contemplative Moments" as a structural rhythm;
  "Let Me Show You" relationship with the viewer; one video's research doc ran to 188 pages. **Weak** (AI summary) — [Podwise](https://podwise.ai/episodes/3299864); [Podwise dashboard listing](https://podwise.ai/dashboard/episodes/3299864)
- A "visual anchor – bridge – visual anchor" formula is attributed to Harris only by an anonymous Reddit comment.
  **Weak** (unverified) — [Reddit mirror](https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/VideoEditing/comments/uik5fu/tips_for_creating_video_essays)

### Inferences
- The channel's five-act clock (Act III starts ~6:00) coincides with the MrBeast "minute 6" boundary: the Boardroom turn
  is a natural re-engagement and should open with a new exhibit, not a recap.
- A re-hook around 3:00 (inside Act II) is worth planning explicitly: e.g. the moment the fail-safe is shown to have a
  hidden dependency.
- Harris's "active prose / contemplative moments" alternation is consistent with the channel's "holds only at act turns
  and signature beats" rule.

### Gaps
- No sourced material was found from LEMMiNO, MagnatesMedia, Wendover, Kurzgesagt on scripting; no 2025–2026 VidCon talk
  transcripts were retrieved. These should be researched before being cited.
- No controlled evidence that re-hooks at fixed minutes raise retention; all of it is practitioner opinion.

## Titles, thumbnails and Test & Compare

### Takeaway
YouTube's own A/B tool picks winners by watch time, not click-through, and now tests titles as well as thumbnails;
practitioners (Muller, Galloway) favour "accurate clickbait": a short, concrete curiosity hook that the video truly
answers, and simpler, less busy thumbnails.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube Help, "Test & compare thumbnails" (page current in 2026): up to 3 titles and thumbnails (titles only, thumbnails
  only, or both); the winner is "the title or combination of title and thumbnail with the highest watch time"; results
  labelled Winner / Performed Same / Inconclusive; if no clear winner, the first uploaded is used; tests complete within
  two weeks; desktop Studio only; not for Shorts, scheduled Lives, Premieres, made-for-kids, mature or private videos.
  YouTube says it optimises "for overall watch time over other metrics, like click-through-rate" because "Great titles and
  thumbnails serve an important purpose beyond getting viewers to click" and this "will best support creators' growth".
  **Strong** (official) — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)
- Earlier label scheme reported by search results: "Winner" (statistically significant on watch time share) and
  "Preferred" (likely better, not significant). The current page wording differs (see above); the labels have changed
  over time. **Medium** — [YouTube Help (search snippet)](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714?hl=com)
- Changing the title or thumbnail by hand stops a running test. **Weak** (third-party guide) — [Tasty Edits](https://www.tastyedits.com/youtube-abc-testing-thumbnails/)
- No public aggregate results from YouTube on how often watch-time winners differ from CTR winners were found. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714)
- Derek Muller, Veritasium, "Clickbait is Unreasonably Effective" (Aug 2021, before the window but the standard reference):
  Type 1 ("legitbait") briefly and accurately conveys the content; Type 2 ("clicktrap") is deceptive or exaggerated and
  invites backlash; the line is vague; packaging takes about as much effort as editing; retitling a video that had 16.3
  million views on outside sites ("Strange Applications of the Magnus Effect" → "Backspin Basketball Flies Off Dam")
  brought steady YouTube growth; the asteroid video only took off (10M+) after a much-changed thumbnail and the simpler
  title "These Are The Asteroids To Worry About"; a topic title ("Collatz problem") reaches only those already interested,
  a hook ("This simple mathematical problem has not yet been solved") reaches more; in a poll, "The Biggest Myth in
  Education" beat "You Are NOT a Visual Learner" overwhelmingly. **Medium** (creator's own data, no CTR figures published)
  — [Gigazine summary, 30 AUG 2021](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20210830-clickbait-effective/)
- Galloway (09 APR 2025): "If they don't click, they don't watch"; a clearer, less busy thumbnail for one creator gave
  roughly 40× more views per day; Red Bull drone-vs-F1 (~28M views) – packaging was the difference between a couple of
  million and 28 million; Veritasium's "96 million black balls" as a curiosity gap vs a duller LA water-supply framing;
  frameworks: superlatives, versus, three-step progressions, exclusive access; a descriptor can beat a name ("the Mozart of
  Gen Z"); write 30–40 title variants per video; most views come from browse and suggested, so think about which videos the
  viewer is watching when yours appears; track AVD, CTR and returning viewers. **Medium** (opinion and anecdote) — [Rosetta transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025)

### Inferences
- Because Test & Compare scores watch time, a misleading thumbnail that wins clicks but loses viewers should lose the test;
  the tool rewards Muller's Type 1 packaging, which suits the channel's honesty rules.
- Thumbnail concepts for a faceless forensic channel can use Galloway's "versus" and "exclusive access" frames honestly:
  the margin vs the money (spec vs measured), and the real exhibit with its record id.
- Each film should run Test & Compare on all three thumbnails (and, separately, title variants) for up to two weeks and
  log results in `films/<slug>/youtube/`, not edit packaging by hand mid-test.

### Gaps
- No sourced study on text on thumbnails, faceless vs face thumbnails, or word count on thumbnails was found in this pass.
- No 2023–2026 Muller statement updating the 2021 video was found.

## Academic evidence: curiosity gaps, clickbait penalties, narrative transportation

### Takeaway
Peer-reviewed evidence does not support the idea that vague curiosity-gap headlines beat clear ones: in news, summary
headlines out-performed curiosity headlines, and clickbait did not reliably create more curiosity; narrative transportation
reliably increases persuasion and is driven by familiar topics and identifiable characters, but none of this was tested on
YouTube.

### Cited Findings
- Scacco & Muddiman (2019/2020), *New Media & Society*, "The curiosity effect": survey of 2,057 people plus a field test of
  5,288 US local political news headlines (2016 elections); "curiosity" headlines (questions, "Why…") got fewer page views
  than "summary" headlines (5,324 vs 6,701 on the reported measure) and participants had lower expectations of them.
  **Strong** (peer-reviewed; pre-window) — [Mapping Ignorance summary](https://mappingignorance.org/?p=6526); [DOI record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1177%2F1461444819863408)
- "Does Clickbait Actually Attract More Clicks? Three Clickbait Studies You Must Read" (CHI 2021): clickbait did not
  generate more curiosity than non-clickbait in two studies; clickbait detectors agreed only 47% of the time.
  **Strong/medium** (peer-reviewed conference; pre-window) — [AMiner record](https://www.aminer.org/pub/60a4e58991e011f4291ce3ac/does-clickbait-actually-attract-more-clicks-three-clickbait-studies-you-must-read)
- Cambridge talk "When Curiosity Gaps Backfire: Effects of Headline Concreteness on Information Selection Decisions" –
  concreteness of the headline matters. **Weak** (talk listing only, findings not read) — [talks.cam.ac.uk](https://talks.cam.ac.uk/talk/index/210730/)
- Qiu & Golman (2024), *Applied Cognitive Psychology*, "Curiosity in news consumption": tests information-gap theory on
  over 100,000 WeChat articles. **Medium** (findings not read in detail) — [Deep Blue](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/items/e4235eeb-79e7-4491-85c9-92425c1fd8d9/full)
- Berger, Moe & Schweidel (2023), *Journal of Marketing*, "What holds attention? Linguistic drivers of engagement" – related
  work on what language keeps readers reading. **Medium** (findings not read here) — [search result summary](https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/items/e4235eeb-79e7-4491-85c9-92425c1fd8d9)
- KQED headline experiment: question headlines were seen as less credible; a 2022 thesis found unexaggerated headlines more
  credible. **Weak/medium** — [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/about/13722/experimenting-with-science-news-headline-format-to-maximize-engagement); [Radboud thesis](https://theses.ubn.ru.nl/handle/123456789/14307)
- van Laer, de Ruyter, Visconti & Wetzels (2014), *Journal of Consumer Research* 40(5), "The Extended
  Transportation-Imagery Model": meta-analysis of 132 effect sizes from 76 articles; narrative transportation increases
  persuasion; topic familiarity and transportability among the strongest influences; 2016 follow-up looks at story domain
  and number of storytellers. **Strong** (pre-window) — [City Research Online](https://openaccess.city.ac.uk/id/eprint/16984/); [core.ac.uk PDF](https://core.ac.uk/download/82917355.pdf)
- A page claiming a "2022 Nielsen Norman Group study" and "2023 HBR analysis" with lift percentages could not be verified;
  excluded. — [search note](https://new.nimss.org/article/how-to-write-clickbait-headlines-that-stop-scrolling)

### Inferences
- The evidence backs specific, concrete titles with a real unknown ("the bolts that were never installed") over vague
  questions ("What really happened?"), and credibility costs for question formats matter more for an audience that
  distrusts PR and outrage.
- Narrative transportation suggests that a concrete, identifiable point of view (the crew, the operator, the inspector, in
  public roles only) increases engagement; it supports human stakes told through the record, not through victims' faces.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study of YouTube retention curves or thumbnail designs (2023–2026) was found in this pass.
- No study on synthetic voice narration and retention or trust was found.

## Forensic and accident storytelling; human stakes; narration pacing

### Takeaway
Very little sourced craft writing exists on accident-documentary structure specifically; the leading aviation channel
(Mentour Pilot) is described as calm, technical and non-sensational, typically telling the sequence first and the causes
second.

### Cited Findings
- Mentour Pilot episodes often follow a two-part pattern: a full synopsis of the accident including the crew's handling,
  then the second half explaining why it happened and the technical aspects; episodes are framed around a narrative
  question. **Weak** (episode descriptions via search) — [TheTVDB season list](https://thetvdb.com/series/mentour-pilot-aviation-accidents-explained/allseasons/official)
- A third-party review describes the channel as calm, technical and non-sensational. **Weak** (opinion) — [Hot Air, 24 MAY 2024](https://hotair.com/david-strom/2024/05/24/things-i-like-mentour-pilot-n3789041)
- Hörnfeldt (CBC, 2024) on a viral fake "I Crashed My Airplane" video: pilots knew at once it was not what it seemed –
  an example of the expert audience detecting staged footage. **Medium** — [CBC As It Happens](https://www.cbc.ca/radio/asithappens/youtube-plane-crash-sentencing-1.7054049?cmp=rss)
- Galloway (2025): open with several questions and promise the answer – a usable device when the cause is already public.
  **Medium** — [Rosetta transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025)

### Inferences
- When the cause is known (public NTSB/CSB finding), the loop can move from "what failed" to "who decided, when, and why
  the margin was there in the first place" – the open question shifts to the Act III document.
- The record as dialogue (transcripts, docket exhibits) suits the "Let Me Show You" stance attributed to Harris and the
  channel's evidence-first look.

### Gaps
- No sourced writing was found on the Swiss-cheese model as a video structure, ticking-clock timelines, or late vs early
  cause reveals in YouTube documentaries.
- No sourced guidance on human stakes without exploiting victims (beyond general ethics) was retrieved.
- No sourced guidance on pacing for synthetic voices (words per minute, pause length) was found.

## Endings, end screens, session continuation and Shorts as discovery

### Takeaway
YouTube has linked Shorts and long-form in recommendations since Aug 2022 and lets a Short carry one "related video" link
(since Sep 2023); third-party 2025–2026 data suggest channels posting some Shorts get more long-form views, but early data
showed few channels getting meaningful long-form traffic from the link, and the effect is unproven.

### Cited Findings
- Todd Beaupré (YouTube director of discovery), Aug 2022: "Recommendations now consider recent long-form videos from
  channels the viewer watches in Shorts"; before that Shorts and long-form had separate histories. **Strong** (official
  statement, pre-window) — [vidIQ, 22 AUG 2022](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-recommends-long-form-videos-shorts-viewers/)
- "Related video" link on Shorts announced by YouTube creator liaison Rene Ritchie, Sep 2023: one link per Short to any
  video on the same channel (long-form, Short or live), set in Studio on desktop. **Strong/medium** — [Tubefilter, 01 SEP 2023](https://tubefilter.com/2023/09/01/youtube-related-links-multi-format-creators-shorts-live-long-form/); [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-adds-shorts-links-limits-links-elsewhere/495463); [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/youtube-enables-linking-shorts-long-form-videos-430655)
- Spotter analysis (early snapshot): only 2% of channels got noticeable long-form traffic from Related Shorts, likely
  because of low adoption. **Weak/medium** — [The Spot](https://spottercommunity.beehiiv.com/p/related-shorts)
- Metricool (study of 799,718 videos, "YouTube Trends 2026"): channels with no Shorts average 4,364 views per long-form
  video vs a 5,985 average; channels posting about 5 Shorts a month average 8,789. Correlational, not causal. **Medium**
  — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- Subscribr notes Shorts viewers who click through often show very low average view duration on long-form. **Weak** —
  [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-hurting-long-form-views)
- Galloway (2025): Shorts worth doing selectively; filter ideas by whether they work as a 20–30 s Short or only as a
  10–20 min video; beware inflated Shorts view counts. **Medium** — [Rosetta transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025)
- YouTube blog "5 ways YouTube Shorts can help superpower your channel" exists as official guidance (contents not read in
  this pass). **Medium** — [blog.youtube](https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/5-ways-youtube-shorts-can-help-superpower-your-channel/)
- Galloway (2025): viewers who stay to the end should get their loops closed; a channel should work like a show with one
  hit format. **Medium** — [Rosetta transcript](https://rosetta.to/u/colinandsamir/the-new-rules-of-youtube-2025)

### Inferences
- Every Short should carry the related-video link to its parent film; the gain is uncertain, the cost is nil.
- Shorts should be cut from a self-contained beat (one mechanism, one exhibit line) that works in 20–30 s, per Galloway's
  filter, rather than as a trailer.
- Close every loop opened in Act I before the sign-off; a "show" identity (consistent format, consistent look) supports
  returning viewers.

### Gaps
- No sourced 2023–2026 guidance on end screens or session-continuation tactics for documentaries was found.
- No 2025–2026 YouTube official statement on Shorts lifting long-form was found; the 2022 Beaupré statement is the latest
  official one located.
